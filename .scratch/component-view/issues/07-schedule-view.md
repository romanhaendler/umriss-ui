# 07: Schedule: the view

**What to build:** A schedule's view is `{ domain, folded }`, handed in through `initialView` (applied by content) and reported whole through `onViewChange`; on `useSchedule`'s return value stand `view`, `setDomain`, `toggleGroup`, `foldAll`, `unfoldAll`. Without a domain in the view the schedule shows its subtasks' extent within `zoomLimits` (confirmed 2026-10-04). `initialDomain`, `collapsedGroups`, `defaultCollapsedGroups`, `onCollapsedGroupsChange` and `onDomainChange` go; the selection stays as it is. The demo gets the page 'View', and 'Linked schedules' keep step through the view.

**2026-10-04, follow-up: no jump back on a late echo.** Two schedules in step through a shared view could jump back mid-pan: A reported v1, and by the time v1 came back as `initialView` A stood at v2 - v1 differed from the last view handed in, so A went to it. The table's rule had the same hazard. Now both keep the views they reported (`Echoes` in `src/view.ts` and `src/model/view.ts`): a view handed in that equals one reported since the last one handed in, within a second, is the component's own state coming back and is not applied. The echoes come in order, so one drops itself and every older report; a view from outside applies and drops them all. "The latest report" alone was not enough - with continuous reports the echo of v1 arrives when v2 is already the latest; "any report since the last view from outside" without dropping would refuse a restore of a view reported earlier. The second bounds the list for an application that never hands back, and lets it still restore a kept view. The schedule decides in its layout effect; the table decides in the render and forgets the answered reports after the commit.
