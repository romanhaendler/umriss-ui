Records under a tree - three ways

An organisation with headcount and hours on every level; a team's employees are records of another kind with more fields. In all three: one chevron per row, always in the first column at its level - the row detail's own expander column is gone. Click the folds and, in C, a team.

A · The records as a sub-list under their team

Opening a team opens its employees right beneath it - no box, no own surface. A rail runs down from the team's fold, a quiet header names the fields, the records are a step smaller. The hours of a person stand under the team's hours.

  • Like a bill of materials or the line items under an account in a financial statement.
  • Library: a leaf's fold opens its RowDetail; a new form of detail, "records", lays the detail rows on the table's grid instead of a free box.

B · The records are the last level of the tree

Employees are rows like units, one level deeper, in the same columns: the name in the first column, the hours under "Hours a week". What has no column of its own - role, since - stands quietly beside the name.

  • Like Jira's or Notion's sub-items, or a file tree where files sit under folders.
  • Library: nothing new - childRows returns the employees for a team, the row type is a union. Cheapest, but only as good as the fields that map onto the units' columns.

C · Tree and records side by side

The tree chooses, the panel shows: click a team and its employees fill the right side with all their columns, sortable, while the tree keeps its place. Opening and choosing are two things - the chevron opens a branch, a click on a team chooses it.

  • Like a mail client, a file manager's column view, or SAP's master-detail layout.
  • Library: tree rows plus a chosen row (an active row, ADR-0003) the application reads; the panel is a second ordinary table.
