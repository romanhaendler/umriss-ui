## 1. Sizes: which named sizes exist, how a widget declares them, how a user changes them, what the guidelines say per size

### Takeaway
All four vendors use a small, closed vocabulary of sizes rather than free pixel sizes: Apple and Microsoft use named families (small/medium/large/extra large; small/medium/large), Android and Home Assistant use integer cell spans with a declared default, minimum and maximum. All of them say the same thing about content: a larger size shows more layers of the same subject, it is not a scaled-up small one, and a widget need not offer every size.

### Cited Findings

**Apple (iOS, iPadOS, macOS, visionOS, watchOS)**

- [V] System family sizes and where they exist: *System small* (iPhone Home Screen, Today View, StandBy, CarPlay; iPad Home Screen, Today View, Lock Screen; Mac desktop and Notification Center; Vision Pro surfaces), *System medium*, *System large* (iPhone, iPad, Mac, Vision Pro), *System extra large* ("Not supported" on iPhone; iPad Home Screen and Today View; Mac desktop and Notification Center; Vision Pro), *System extra large portrait* (Vision Pro only). — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] Accessory sizes: *Accessory circular*, *Accessory rectangular*, *Accessory inline* (iPhone and iPad Lock Screen; Apple Watch complications; circular and rectangular also in the watch Smart Stack) and *Accessory corner* (Apple Watch complications only). — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] Sizes are fixed per device, not user-scalable. Example iPhone 430×932 pt: small 170×170, medium 364×170, large 364×382 pt. iPhone 393×852 pt: 158×158, 338×158, 338×354. iPad 1024×1366 pt (device): small 160×160, medium 356×160, large 356×356, extra large 748×356. watchOS Smart Stack widget: 152×69.5 pt (40 mm) to 191×81.5 pt (49 mm). — [Apple HIG, Widgets, Specifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] visionOS is the one Apple platform with continuous scaling: "people can scale a widget from 75 to 125 percent in size." — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] Per-size content guidance: "Offer widgets in multiple sizes when doing so adds value. Small widgets use their limited space to typically show a single piece of information while larger sizes support additional layers of information and actions. Avoid expanding a smaller widget’s content to simply fill a larger area. It’s more important to create one widget in the size that best represents the content than providing the widget in all sizes." — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] "Balance information density. Sparse layouts can make the widget seem unnecessary, while overly dense layouts are less glanceable. […] If your layout is too dense, consider improving its clarity by using a larger widget size or replacing text with graphics." — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] "In each size, the widget remains centered on events while expanding the range of information as the size increases." (Calendar example) — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] The gallery treats sizes as one widget: "Group your widget’s sizes together, and provide a single description. If your widget is available in multiple sizes, group them together so people don’t think each size is a different widget." — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] WWDC 2020 (iOS 14 introduction), Christian, Apple HI team: "The new system supports three sizes that you can design with: small, medium and large." and "When you're designing for each size make sure not to scale up your smaller widget into your larger widget. Think about the information you're working with and what makes the most sense for each size." and "If you don't have more information to show in your larger sizes though, it's fine to only support specific sizes of your idea." and "The small widget supports a single tap target." — [WWDC 2020 session 10103, Design great widgets](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10103/)
- [V] How a user changes size today (guide version iOS 27): "Touch and hold the app icon or widget on your Home Screen. Choose a widget size. Note: Not all apps offer every widget size. Some apps provide only small and medium widgets, while others offer sizes up to extra large." The same menu turns an app icon into a widget and back. — [Apple, Customize apps and widgets on the iPhone Home Screen](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/customize-apps-and-widgets-on-the-home-screen-iph385473442/ios)
- [V] iOS 18 (Sept 2024) introduced in-place resizing; before it, a size change meant delete and re-add. MacStories describes the old state as "the need to delete an existing widget if you want to replace it with the same one of a different size", the new menu as shape outlines below "Edit Widget" showing only the sizes the widget supports, and the handle: "if you're in jiggle mode, a drag handle will appear next to widgets that support resizing". Three sizes on iPhone, four on iPad. — [MacStories, iOS and iPadOS 18 review, Home Screen chapter](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-18-the-macstories-review/2)
- [V] The size vocabulary did not grow: "On iOS, we've had the same small/medium/large configuration since iOS 14"; the reviewer asks for more shapes (an extra-small rectangle, column-shaped widgets). — [MacStories, iOS and iPadOS 18 review](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-18-the-macstories-review/2)
- [V] macOS: size is changed from the context menu: "Control-click a widget. […] Change the size of the widget: Choose a different size." — [Apple, Add and customize widgets on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/add-and-customize-widgets-mchl52be5da5/mac)
- [V] watchOS Smart Stack: one widget shape; Apple's design team "has created six design layouts" (three-line text, colour-coded, bar gauge, circular graphic, large text, charts) plus a Combo widget with three circular complications. — [WWDC23 session 10309, Design widgets for the Smart Stack on Apple Watch](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10309/)

**Android (Android 12+, Material You)**

- [V] A widget declares cell spans plus dp bounds in its `appwidget-provider` XML. Android 12 (API 31) added `targetCellWidth` / `targetCellHeight`, which "define the target size of the widget in terms of launcher grid cells. If defined, these attributes are used instead of `minWidth` or `minHeight`", and `maxResizeWidth` / `maxResizeHeight`, which "define the maximum size that the launcher allows the user to resize the widget to." Older attributes: `minWidth`, `minHeight`, `minResizeWidth`, `minResizeHeight`. — [Android Developers, Provide flexible widget layouts](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/appwidgets/layouts)
- [V] "Each widget must define a `targetCellWidth` and `targetCellHeight` for devices running Android 12 or higher—or `minWidth` and `minHeight` for all versions of Android—indicating the minimum amount of space it consumes by default." — [Android Developers, layouts](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/appwidgets/layouts)
- [V] Cell-to-dp estimate on a Pixel 4 with a 5×4 grid: portrait (73n − 16) × (118m − 16) dp, landscape (142n − 15) × (66m − 15) dp; 1×1 = 57×102 dp portrait, 127×51 dp landscape. "Cell dimensions and automatic margins vary across devices." — [Android Developers, layouts](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/appwidgets/layouts)
- [V] Content per size is done with breakpoints, not scaling: "If the layout needs to change depending on the size of the widget, we recommend creating a small set of layouts, each valid for a range of sizes." Example mapping `SizeF(180,110)→small`, `SizeF(270,110)→medium`, `SizeF(270,280)→large`. The fallback, exact layouts, is "typically two sizes for phones (portrait and landscape mode) and four sizes for foldables." — [Android Developers, layouts](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/appwidgets/layouts)
- [V] Recommended sizes (Pixel-based). Handheld: 2×1, 2×2, 2×3, 4×1, 4×2, 4×3 (e.g. 2×2 = 109–306 dp wide, 115–276 dp high; 4×2 = 245–624 × 115–276 dp). Tablet: 2×1, 2×2, 2×3, 3×1, 3×2, 3×3, 3×4. "Deliver a polished widget experience by optimizing your layout for at least one of our recommended sizes." — [Android design guide, Widget sizing](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/widgets/sizing)
- [V] "Breakpoints also offer the flexibility to conditionally include or exclude supplemental content, optimizing space utilization based on the widget's dimensions." — [Android design guide, Widget sizing](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/widgets/sizing)
- [V] Stated reason users remove widgets: "One of the primary reasons users remove widgets is due to misalignment with other home screen elements. To prevent this, ensure your widget always fills its allocated grid space completely." Don't: "Add custom padding." Don't: "Use fixed square shapes. Instead, use responsive rectangular containers that adapt to various grid dimensions." — [Android design guide, Widget sizing](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/widgets/sizing)
- [V] Per form factor defaults: Don't "Set the same default sizes across all device sizes, make sure to set specific sizes based of each form factors grid sizes." Also don't "Change the widget's shape or size when the user initially drops it onto the home screen". — [Android design guide, Discovery and promotion](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/widgets/discovery-promotion)
- [V] How the user resizes: "Touch and hold the widget on your Home screen. Lift your finger. If the widget can be resized, an outline with dots is visible on the sides. Drag the dots to resize. Tap outside the widget when you're done." — [Android Help, Add apps, shortcuts & widgets](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9450271?hl=en)
- [V] Samsung One UI: resizable widgets show a dot on their outline; "Touch and hold the dot and drag it to resize the widget." — [Samsung, Add, customize, and use widgets on Galaxy phones](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10000945/)

**Windows 11 (Widgets Board)**

- [V] "A single widget can support three different sizes; small, medium, and large. […] For each size from small to large, the purpose of the widget should remain the same, but the amount of information displayed should expand with larger sizes. We recommend that widget providers implement all widget sizes to give users flexibility when customizing the widget layout." — [Microsoft Learn, Windows Widgets](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/widgets/) (page dated 5 Jul 2026)
- [V] Per size: small "should not try to force all of the functionality that could comfortably fit in a large widget. Focus on one user interaction or piece of key information that can be surfaced here with 1 touch target." Medium: "2-3 touch targets." Large: "no more than 3-4 touch targets." — [Microsoft Learn, Widget design fundamentals](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/widgets/widgets-design-fundamentals) (page dated 29 Aug 2026)
- [V] "If you have a lot of information to include, consider the next size up to show more content." — [Microsoft Learn, Widget states and built-in UI components](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/widgets/widgets-states-and-ui)
- [V] The size switch is system UI, not the widget's: "The context menu is displayed when the user clicks on the three-dot icon on the top right. This menu allows users to select their preferred widget size and access the widget's configuration state." User-facing: choose Small, Medium or Large; "Some widgets might only offer one or two sizes." — [Microsoft Learn, states and UI](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/widgets/widgets-states-and-ui); [Microsoft Support, Stay up to date with Widgets](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/stay-up-to-date-with-widgets-7ba79aaa-dac6-4687-b460-ad16a06be6e4)
- [V] Fixed chrome per widget: "Each widget has a 16px margin around it and a 48px Attribution area in which content cannot be placed." — [Microsoft Learn, Widget design fundamentals](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/widgets/widgets-design-fundamentals)

**Home Assistant (card sizes; detail in section 7)**

- [V] A card declares default, minimum and maximum spans through `getGridOptions()`: `columns`, `rows`, `min_columns`, `max_columns`, `min_rows`, `max_rows`; `columns: "full"` forces full section width. "Each section is divided in 12 columns." Cell: width = section width / 12 ("approximately `30px`"), height `56px`, gap `8px`. "it's `highly` recommended to use multiple of 3 for the default value (`3`, `6`, `9` or `12`)". Without the method "the card will take 12 columns and will ignore the rows of the grid." — [Home Assistant developer docs, Custom card](https://developers.home-assistant.io/docs/frontend/custom-ui/custom-card/)

### Inferences
- Two declaration models exist and both work: an enumerated set of families (Apple, Microsoft) and a span range with a default (Android, Home Assistant). The second maps naturally onto a CSS grid; the first is simpler for the widget author because every size is a known layout.
- Every platform puts the size switch in system chrome (context menu, handle, layout tab) and lets the widget only declare what it supports; the user is never offered a size the widget did not declare.
- Home Assistant's "multiples of 3 on a 12-column grid" is the same idea as Android's "recommended sizes": a fine grid exists, but the design system steers to a few coarse steps so neighbouring widgets line up.
- "A widget need not support every size" is explicit at Apple and at Microsoft ("might only offer one or two sizes"); a library should let a widget type declare a subset.

### Gaps
- Windows widget pixel dimensions per size are not on the Microsoft Learn pages read. (From memory, unverified: 300 px wide; small ≈ 146 px, medium ≈ 304 px, large ≈ 624 px high.)
- Android's `resizeMode` attribute (horizontal / vertical / none) is not mentioned on the layouts page read; known from memory only.
- Whether the iOS 18 resize handle is still present in iOS 26 and iOS 27 was not verified: Apple's current guide documents only the touch-and-hold size menu, not the handle. The iPhone guide sentence "others offer sizes up to extra large" conflicts with the HIG table, which lists extra large as "Not supported" on iPhone; likely shared wording with the iPad guide.
- The iPhone icon grid in cells (commonly given as 4 columns × 6 rows, small widget 2×2, medium 4×2, large 4×4 icon cells) was not confirmed against an Apple primary source in this session; the HIG gives point dimensions only.

## 2. Placement: grid, flow, gaps, what happens when a dragged widget meets others, rotation and other screen sizes

### Takeaway
Three placement models are in the field: forced packing in reading order (iOS before 18, Home Assistant's default "Z-Grid"), free placement on a cell grid with gaps (Android since the start, iOS since 18, macOS desktop), and a managed board (Windows). No vendor keeps one stored arrangement that is spatially identical on every screen width: Apple and Google hold a layout per device (and iPadOS reshuffles on rotation, to lasting complaints), Samsung foldables offer mirror-or-separate, and only Home Assistant designed the reflow itself (blocks move, the inside of a block does not).

### Cited Findings

**iOS before 18: packing, no gaps**

- [V] The old rule as MacStories states it: "There cannot be empty spots between icons, and users cannot create empty rows or columns anywhere"; users resorted to blank-icon utilities to fake gaps. — [MacStories, iOS and iPadOS 18 review](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-18-the-macstories-review/2)
- [S] iOS 14 user reaction (forum anecdote, 2020): adding a widget "can mess your carefully designed arrangement pretty badly which will take careful manipulations to fix"; also difficulty telling a stack from a single widget at a glance. — numericcitizen.me blog, via search digest: [link](https://blog.numericcitizen.me/categories/apple--tech/page/127/)

**iOS 18 (Sept 2024) and later: free placement on the same grid**

- [V] Apple's announcement, 10 June 2024: "Users can now arrange apps and widgets in any open space on the Home Screen", "including placing them right above the dock for easy access or perfectly framing a wallpaper". — [Apple Newsroom, iOS 18](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/06/ios-18-makes-iphone-more-personal-capable-and-intelligent-than-ever/)
- [V] Current user guide (iOS 27): "You can move apps and widgets anywhere on the Home Screen to create the ideal layout—frame your wallpaper photo, align apps and widgets along the side or bottom, or drag them to other Home Screen pages. If you have multiple Home Screen pages, each one can have a unique layout." — [Apple, Move apps and widgets on the iPhone Home Screen](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/move-apps-and-widgets-on-the-home-screen-iphd2fc8ce30/ios)
- [V] Reviewer verdict: "I consider the more dynamic iOS 18 Home Screen layout a success"; "Apple managed to keep the ideological sanctity of the iPhone's icon grid while opening it up to users' creativity"; the system "keeps the underlying grid system, so it's difficult to make something that looks bad or chaotic". — [MacStories, iOS and iPadOS 18 review](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-18-the-macstories-review/2)
- [V] Reviewer criticism: "There still are, however, arbitrary limitations on how icons and widgets can be moved around on the Home Screen"; "On the iPhone, you can't place a small widget between two columns of icons"; moving a widget next to a column can push a whole group of icons to the opposite side (paraphrase). — [MacStories, iOS and iPadOS 18 review](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-18-the-macstories-review/2)
- [S] Other reviewers on the same point: once something occupies the top-left slot, neighbours are pulled back into the auto-aligning grid and moving one icon can shift the rest; an app dragged from search or the App Library to an empty page jumps to the top-left corner; a 2×2 widget cannot sit centred beside a column of apps. — Tom's Guide and Digital Trends via search digest: [Tom's Guide](https://tomsguide.com/phones/iphones/this-is-my-favorite-ios-18-feature-and-it-has-nothing-to-do-with-apple-intelligence), [Digital Trends (URL as returned by the search, a staging host)](https://qa2.www.digitaltrends.com/mobile/i-thought-id-love-these-two-ios-18-features-but-i-dont)

**iPadOS: rotation reshuffles**

- [V] iPadOS 15 (2021) put widgets among the icons and stopped keeping one grid for both orientations: "the icon grid changes from 6 x 4 to 4 x 6"; "your iPad Home screen icons have to make room for your widgets whenever you rotate your iPad"; without widgets "Some of your icons will be pushed to a different row"; "Unfortunately, there's not yet a way to change the new Home screen icon grid." The article's only fix is Rotation Lock. — [iPhone Life, 17 Jan 2024](https://iphonelife.com/content/ipados-15-wrecked-your-ipad-home-screen-icons-heres-how-to-fix-it)
- [S] MacStories' iPadOS 15 review said iPadOS 15 "no longer keeps the same icon grid layout in portrait and landscape orientations" and hoped for a setting to restore the consistent layout; Apple Support Community threads on this run from 2021 to 2025. — search digest: [Apple Community thread](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256131088)

**macOS (Sonoma 14, 2023, and later): desktop widgets**

- [V] Two ways to place, both offered side by side: "Automatically position a widget on the desktop: Click the widget (or click the widget’s Add button). Manually position a widget on the desktop: Drag the widget to any position on the desktop." Notification Center is a single column: "To change the new widget’s location in Notification Center, drag it up or down." — [Apple, Add and customize widgets on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/add-and-customize-widgets-mchl52be5da5/mac)

**Android: free placement on a cell grid, live make-room**

- [V] "Android home screens offer users a grid of available spaces […] This grid can vary by device; for example, many handsets offer a 5x4 grid, and tablets can offer a larger grid." — [Android Developers, layouts](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/appwidgets/layouts)
- [V] Move: "Touch and drag the widget. Thumbnails of your Home screens appear. Slide it to the new spot. Lift your finger." — [Android Help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9450271?hl=en)
- [V] What the AOSP launcher does when a dragged item meets others, in the order the source tries it (comments in `ReorderAlgorithm.java`, copyright 2023, extracted from `CellLayout`): (1) "We find the nearest cell into which we would place the dragged item, assuming there's nothing in its way." (2) "First we try to find a solution which respects the push mechanic. That is, we try to find a solution such that no displaced item travels through another item without also displacing that item." (3) "Next we try moving the views as a block, but without requiring the push mechanic." (4) "Ok, they couldn't move as a block, let's move them individually". Fallbacks: "Returns a 'reorder' where we simply drop the item in the closest empty space, without moving any other item in the way." The widget being dropped may also be shrunk: "We try shrinking the widget down to size in an alternating pattern, shrink 1 in x, then 1 in y etc.", but "If the reorder solution requires resizing (shrinking) the item being dropped, we instead favor a solution in which the item is not resized". — [AOSP Launcher3, ReorderAlgorithm.java](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/apps/Launcher3/+/refs/heads/main/src/com/android/launcher3/celllayout/ReorderAlgorithm.java)
- [V] Same source, on determinism and feel: "The views need to be sorted so that the results are deterministic on the views positions and not by the views hash which is 'random'." and, on the push direction, "we use some heuristics to often lock the vector to up, down, left or right, which helps make pushing feel right." A failed attempt is rolled back: "if we fail to find a solution we will revert to the initial state." — [AOSP Launcher3, ReorderAlgorithm.java](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/apps/Launcher3/+/refs/heads/main/src/com/android/launcher3/celllayout/ReorderAlgorithm.java)
- [V] Android's widget tables are stated to "encompass all device orientations, including landscape mode on phones"; a widget must survive a landscape cell that is wide and flat (1×1 = 127×51 dp on a Pixel 4). — [Android design guide, Widget sizing](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/widgets/sizing); [Android Developers, layouts](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/appwidgets/layouts)
- [S] Foldables: Samsung offers "Cover screen mirroring" as a Home screen setting (Z Fold3 to Z Fold7); with it on, cover and main screen share one layout and one main-screen page maps to two cover-screen pages; with it off each screen has its own layout, wallpaper, widgets and folders, and turning mirroring off restores the main screen's earlier layout. — Samsung support and SamMobile via search digest: [Samsung, foldable opened or closed](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS00083025), [Samsung, Customize the screens on your Galaxy Z Fold5](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10003695/)

**Windows 11: a managed board**

- [V] "Installed widgets are displayed in a grid in the Widgets Board"; "The Widgets Board displays widgets and manages their layout on the board." The user moves a widget by its header "within the pinned section"; adding a widget keeps it in "a predictable and easily accessible location." — [Microsoft Learn, Windows Widgets](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/widgets/); [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/stay-up-to-date-with-widgets-7ba79aaa-dac6-4687-b460-ad16a06be6e4)
- [S] The board was restructured repeatedly: a 2024 test (24H2) added a left navigation pane separating "My Widgets" from "My Feed"; a later redesign added multiple dashboards with a Copilot-curated Discover feed. Standing criticism: cluttered board, low-quality MSN feed, resizing only through menus. — search digest: [Windows Latest, 29 Mar 2024](https://windowslatest.com/2024/03/29/windows-11-tests-a-new-widget-board-layout-with-a-navigation-pane/), [ITdaily](https://itdaily.com/news/software/windows-11-redesigned-widgets-board), [Thurrott, Windows 11 Field Guide](https://www.thurrott.com/books/windows-11-field-guide/311698/widgets-24h2)

**Home Assistant**: see section 7 (Z-Grid of sections, dense placement, reflow).

### Inferences
- iOS 14 to 17 shows the cost of pure packing: one insertion re-flows everything after it, and users invented blank icons to get gaps. iOS 18 shows the cost of a half-step: gaps are allowed, yet residual packing rules surprise reviewers ("arbitrary limitations"). The lesson for a library is to choose one model and make its rule statable in one sentence.
- Android's launcher is the most explicit model of live make-room: nearest cell first, then a physical "push" (nothing passes through anything), then block move, then individual moves, then closest empty space; deterministic ordering; revert on failure. This is a ready-made specification for drag behaviour on a coordinate grid.
- Nobody on the OS side solved "one stored arrangement at every width". Phones avoid the question (one orientation, one grid per device); iPadOS re-flows and is criticised for it five years on; Samsung asks the user to choose mirroring or separate layouts. Home Assistant is the only one that designed for it, by making the unit of reflow coarser than the unit of arrangement.
- Windows demonstrates the opposite pole: when the host "manages their layout", the user's control is limited to order and three sizes, and criticism moves to what the host injects (the feed), not to layout.

### Gaps
- No Apple statement explaining *why* gaps were disallowed until iOS 18 or why they were allowed then, beyond the newsroom sentence about framing wallpaper, was found.
- Whether iPadOS 18, 26 or 27 changed the rotation behaviour introduced with iPadOS 15 was not verified.
- Pixel Fold / Pixel Tablet handling of one layout across inner and outer screen was not found in the sources read.
- How iOS animates neighbours during a drag (order of displacement) is not documented by Apple; only reviewers' observations exist.
- The exact layout rules of the Windows 11 pinned section (column count, packing) are not documented on the pages read.

## 3. Edit mode: entry, what it shows, exit, whether changes apply at once, undo

### Takeaway
Apple and Home Assistant have an explicit mode with a named exit; Android and Windows have none, the object itself is the handle. Everywhere a change applies the moment it is made; no platform read here offers an undo for arrangement, the nearest things being a per-card "revert" for size in Home Assistant and a whole-layout reset on iOS.

### Cited Findings
- [V] iOS entry and exit: "Touch and hold the Home Screen background until the items begin to jiggle. […] When you’re finished, press the Home button (on an iPhone with a Home button) or tap the Home Screen background (on other iPhone models)." Adding goes through the mode: "Touch and hold the background until the apps begin to jiggle, tap [Edit], then tap Add Widget." — [Apple, Move apps and widgets](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/move-apps-and-widgets-on-the-home-screen-iphd2fc8ce30/ios); [Apple, Add, edit, and remove widgets on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/add-edit-and-remove-widgets-iphb8f1bf206/ios)
- [V] WWDC 2020: "In iOS 14, your widgets will jiggle just like apps do in edit mode and you can tap on a widget here to flip it around and see what it allows for you to edit." — [WWDC 2020 session 10103](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10103/)
- [V] In jiggle mode iOS 18 shows a resize handle on resizable widgets (MacStories, quoted in section 1). — [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-18-the-macstories-review/2)
- [V] The only "undo" Apple documents is a full reset: Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone > Reset > "Reset Home Screen Layout"; "Any folders you created are removed, and apps you downloaded are arranged alphabetically after apps that came with your iPhone." — [Apple, Move apps and widgets](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/move-apps-and-widgets-on-the-home-screen-iphd2fc8ce30/ios)
- [V] macOS: entry "Control-click the wallpaper, then choose Edit Widgets"; each widget then shows a Remove button; exit "click Done at the bottom-right corner of the widget gallery." Outside the mode a widget's context menu still offers Edit, size and Remove. — [Apple, Add and customize widgets on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/add-and-customize-widgets-mchl52be5da5/mac)
- [V] Android has no global mode: a long press on one widget selects it; lifting the finger shows "an outline with dots" for resizing, dragging moves it, dragging "up to Remove" removes it; "Tap outside the widget when you're done." — [Android Help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9450271?hl=en)
- [V] Windows has no mode either: drag by the widget header; the three-dot menu holds size, "Customize widget" and "Remove widget". — [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/stay-up-to-date-with-widgets-7ba79aaa-dac6-4687-b460-ad16a06be6e4)
- [V] Home Assistant: "Select the edit button in the top right corner of the dashboard"; in the mode sections show a move button and cards are dragged by tap-and-hold; leaving is "Done" in the top right. A size change has its own revert: "If you mess up the look of a card, luckily we have included a revert button in the top left corner". — [Home Assistant, Sections view](https://www.home-assistant.io/dashboards/sections/); [Home Assistant, Cards](https://www.home-assistant.io/dashboards/cards/); [Home Assistant 2024.7 release notes](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/07/03/release-20247/)
- [V] Home Assistant 2025.10 added undo and redo ("up to 75 steps", Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y) to the automation editor and to the YAML and template code editors; the release notes do not mention the dashboard editor. — [Home Assistant 2025.10 release notes](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/10/01/release-202510/)

### Inferences
- A mode is used where the surface is also the launch surface (a tap must open an app, so moving needs a different state): iOS, macOS desktop, Home Assistant. Where a long press on the object is free, no mode is needed (Android). A data-dense web dashboard whose widgets are themselves interactive is in the first group.
- No platform has a "Cancel" that discards a session of arrangement changes. "Done" only leaves the mode. A library that offers cancel or undo would go beyond the consumer models, which matters because a business dashboard holds more work than a home screen.
- Home Assistant's per-card revert shows the cheapest useful undo: back to the declared default size.

### Gaps
- Whether Home Assistant's dashboard editor received undo/redo after 2025.10 was not verified.
- Whether Home Assistant persists each drag immediately or on "Done" is not stated unambiguously in the docs read (the Cards page says to select Done "to save the view").
- No source on undo for widget arrangement on Android, iOS or Windows was found; absence is inferred from the documentation, not stated by the vendors.

## 4. Adding: gallery, preview per size, tap versus drag, where it lands, configuration and defaults

### Takeaway
The gallery pattern is uniform: search plus suggestions, a live-looking preview for every size of one widget, and two ways to add (tap for automatic placement, drag for a chosen place). All vendors insist that a freshly added widget shows useful content without configuration; configuration is a later, optional step reached from the widget itself.

### Cited Findings

**Gallery and preview**

- [V] iOS: "Scroll or search to find the widget you want, tap it, then swipe through the size and style options. When you see the size you want, tap Add Widget. While the apps are still jiggling, move the widget where you want it, then tap [Done]." — [Apple, Add, edit, and remove widgets on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/add-edit-and-remove-widgets-iphb8f1bf206/ios)
- [V] iOS shortcut that skips the gallery: "Touch and hold the app icon on your Home screen. To choose the size of the widget, tap one of the icons at the bottom of the pop-up menu." — [Apple, Add, edit, and remove widgets on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/add-edit-and-remove-widgets-iphb8f1bf206/ios)
- [V] "Design a realistic preview to display in the widget gallery. Highlighting your widget’s capabilities — and clearly representing the experiences each widget type or size can provide — helps people make an informed decision. You can display real data in your widget preview, but if the data takes too long to generate or load, display realistic simulated data instead." — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] Description wording: "Begin a description with an action verb […] Avoid including unnecessary phrases that reference the widget itself, like 'This widget shows…'". — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] macOS: "In the widget gallery, search for a widget. Or click a category to view its available widgets." Click = automatic position, drag = manual position. — [Apple, Add and customize widgets on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/add-and-customize-widgets-mchl52be5da5/mac)
- [V] Android picker: "Users discover and add your widget through the widget picker, as shown from the Widgets menu that appears when they long-press on the home screen or on your app icon. On some devices, the widget picker displays a suggestions section that shows quality widgets." and "Imagine the widget picker as a shopping window. Your widget's default size is its initial display". Don't: "Leave out a preview, as this will cause the widget preview to show only an image app icon." — [Android design guide, Discovery and promotion](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/widgets/discovery-promotion)
- [V] Android against one picker entry per variant: "Avoid publishing multiple versions of the same widget size solely to offer different colors or shapes. Instead, incorporate a configuration activity within the widget". — [Android design guide, Discovery and promotion](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/widgets/discovery-promotion)
- [V] Android 12 (API 31) "enables you to improve the widget picker experience for your app by adding dynamic widget previews and widget descriptions." — [Android Developers, Android 12 widgets improvements](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/12/features/widgets)
- [V] Android was drag-only until Android 15: tapping a widget in the picker showed the toast "Touch & hold to move a widget."; Android 15 Beta 2 (May 2024) added an "Add" button on tap; if the current home screen has no room the widget goes to "the next available space"; dragging still works. — [9to5Google, Abner Li, 21 May 2024](https://9to5google.com/2024/05/21/android-15-add-widget/); toast string confirmed in [AOSP Launcher3 strings.xml](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/apps/Launcher3/+/refs/heads/main/res/values/strings.xml)
- [V] Samsung: "Touch and hold the Home screen, then tap Widgets. Recommended widgets appear at the top. […] Select a widget and choose an option (options vary by widget), then tap Add." (paraphrase of steps) — [Samsung, widgets on Galaxy phones](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10000945/)
- [V] Windows: the "+" button opens the widget picker, which shows installed widgets before adding. — [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/stay-up-to-date-with-widgets-7ba79aaa-dac6-4687-b460-ad16a06be6e4)
- [V] Home Assistant: add from a section's add control; "By entity: Select the entities from the list" then pick a card from the preview, or "By card: Browse the list of available cards", with a "Suggested cards" group; also "Add to Dashboard" from a device page, which proposes a card the user may accept or replace ("Pick different card"). — [Home Assistant, Cards](https://www.home-assistant.io/dashboards/cards/)

**Configuration and "never empty"**

- [V] Apple: "Choose between automatically displaying content and letting people customize displayed information. In some cases, people need to configure a widget to ensure it displays the information that’s most useful for them. For example, the Stocks widget lets people select the stocks they wish to track. In contrast, some widgets — like the Podcasts widget — automatically display recent content, so people don’t need to customize them." — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] Apple, how the user configures: "Touch and hold the widget. Tap Edit Widget (or Edit Stack, if it’s a Smart Stack), then choose options. […] Tap Done." On Mac: "Choose Edit [widget name], then change options or click highlighted information to change it. […] Note: If Edit [widget name] isn’t available in the shortcut menu, you can’t change what the widget shows." — [Apple iPhone guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/add-edit-and-remove-widgets-iphb8f1bf206/ios); [Apple Mac guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/add-and-customize-widgets-mchl52be5da5/mac)
- [V] Apple, why per-instance configuration exists (WWDC 2020, Mac, HI team): people can add several instances and configure each, which "eliminates the need to create complex widget layouts that represent different entities of information combined into a single widget." — [WWDC 2020 session 10103](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10103/)
- [V] Apple placeholder: "An installed widget displays placeholder content while its data loads. Create an effective placeholder appearance by combining static interface components with semi-opaque shapes that stand in for dynamic content." WWDC 2020: "Every widget must provide a placeholder". — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets); [WWDC 2020 session 10103](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10103/)
- [V] Apple signed-out state: "an app that shows upcoming reservations might include a message like 'Sign in to view reservations' when people are signed out." — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] Android default configuration (Android 12+): "You can provide a more seamless widget experience by letting users skip the initial configuration step. To do this, specify both the `configuration_optional` and `reconfigurable` flags in the `widgetFeatures` field. This bypasses launching the configuration activity after a user adds the widget. […] For example, a clock widget can bypass the initial configuration and show the device time zone by default." — [Android Developers, Enable users to configure app widgets (Glance)](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/glance/configuration)
- [V] Android reconfiguration: "Users can reconfigure their widget by touching & holding the widget and tapping the Reconfigure button" (shown at the widget's bottom-right corner); requires `android:widgetFeatures="reconfigurable"`. "These options are only available starting in Android 12 (API level 31)." The launcher labels the button "Change widget settings". — [Android Developers, Glance configuration](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/glance/configuration); [AOSP Launcher3 strings.xml](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/apps/Launcher3/+/refs/heads/main/res/values/strings.xml)
- [V] Windows: "your widget's default state should be fully implemented and should not be empty before user configuration." Also: "Consider using the user’s location to pre-populate the data […] instead of generic data." Customization: "Starting with Windows App SDK 1.4, widgets can provide a customization template that provides controls for the user to customize the widget's appearance or the data displayed by the widget." Error state is the host's: "Windows will show the widget header with an error message and a reload button. This message will look the same for every widget." — [Microsoft Learn, Widget states and built-in UI components](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/widgets/widgets-states-and-ui)

### Inferences
- The stable pattern is: add first with a sensible default, configure afterwards from the widget's own menu. Android moved to this in Android 12 after years of a mandatory configuration screen on drop; Windows wrote it into its rules; Apple distinguishes widgets that need configuration from those that never do.
- Tap-to-add with automatic placement is now universal (Android added it last, in Android 15). Drag-to-place remains as the precise alternative. A library should offer both and define "where a tapped widget lands" (macOS: "automatically position"; Android: "the next available space").
- States a host should own rather than each widget: loading placeholder, error with reload, signed-out. Microsoft makes error identical for every widget.
- The gallery shows one entry per widget type with its sizes grouped, not one entry per size or colour variant (Apple and Android say so explicitly).

### Gaps
- Where exactly a tapped widget lands on iOS (first free slot on the current page versus top of page) is not stated in Apple's guide.
- No source read describes gallery search ranking or how "suggested" widgets are chosen on any platform.

## 5. Stacks: iOS Smart Stack and widget stacks, Samsung stacks, watchOS Smart Stack

### Takeaway
A stack trades space for time: several widgets of one size share one slot and the user flicks between them, optionally with the system rotating by relevance. The automatic rotation is the contested part; Samsung removed it, and watchOS pairs it with pinning so the user can make the stack "as dynamic or as fixed" as they want.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Apple's definition: "A Smart Stack (a widget with dots next to it) is a combined set of widgets that uses information such as the time, your location, and activity to automatically display the most relevant widget at the appropriate time in your day. You can add a Smart Stack from the widget gallery to the Home Screen, then swipe up and down through it to see the widgets it contains." Editing: "For a Smart Stack, you can reorder widgets by dragging them within the stack, drag a widget from the Smart Stack to the Home Screen, or remove a widget". — [Apple, Add, edit, and remove widgets on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/add-edit-and-remove-widgets-iphb8f1bf206/ios)
- [V] WWDC 2020 rationale (Mac, HI team): "Smart Stacks. These let you add several different widgets into a single location and quickly flick between the different widgets inside." and "a smart stack will automatically rotate to show you the most relevant widget at a given time. So the information you're looking for is already there before you even have to lift a finger." — [WWDC 2020 session 10103](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10103/)
- [S] iOS criticism, from how-to articles rather than reviews: stacks created by hand have "Smart Rotate" on by default; a separate "Widget Suggestions" toggle makes iOS add widgets the user did not put there; "it might be jarring to come back to your iPhone with all your widgets showing something different." Both toggles sit under Edit Stack. — search digest: [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/692398/how-to-stop-iphone-widget-stacks-from-automatically-changing/), [iPhone Life](https://iphonelife.com/content/random-widgets-smart-stacks)
- [V] watchOS 10 (2023): "the Smart Stack intelligently orders the widgets in it so that the most relevant one floats to the top"; relevance moments are an imminent time, a reached location, headphones detected, waking or going to bed, starting or ending a workout. "Smart Stack also provides people with the ability to manually add, remove, and pin their widgets to the top. This means that the Smart Stack can be as dynamic or as fixed as a person wants." — [WWDC23 session 10309](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10309/)
- [V] The HIG describes the watch position as placing a widget "At a fixed position in the Smart Stack of Apple Watch" and tells developers to supply relevance so the system can "display or elevate the position of your watchOS widget". — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] Samsung: "Touch and hold your desired widget on the Home screen, and then tap Create stack."; "Swipe left or right on the stack to switch between them and view their information."; Edit stack adds with a plus and removes with a red minus. Rotation was withdrawn: "Auto rotate is not available on OneUI 6.1.1 or higher." — [Samsung, widgets on Galaxy phones](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10000945/)
- [S] Samsung history: stacks arrived as "Smart Widgets" in One UI 4.1 (2022); from One UI 5.0 a widget can be dropped onto another of the same size to form a stack; a community post gives a limit of 7 widgets per stack (not confirmed by Samsung). — search digest: [9to5Google, 25 Mar 2022](https://9to5google.com/2022/03/25/create-smart-widgets-on-your-samsung-galaxy-smartphone-heres-how/), [Samsung Community](https://r2.community.samsung.com/t5/Tech-Talk/One-UI-Tip-Declutter-with-Stacked-Widgets/td-p/22304416)

### Inferences
- The problem a stack solves is scarcity of a small screen. A data-dense desktop dashboard has a different scarcity (attention, not pixels); a stack hides data behind a swipe, which works against glanceability on a large surface. Tabs inside one widget are the nearer web equivalent.
- System-driven reordering undermines exactly the spatial memory the rest of the arrangement protects. Samsung dropped auto rotate; Apple keeps it but made it switchable and, on the watch, pinnable. If a library ever adds stacks, user order should be the default and rotation opt-in.

### Gaps
- No Samsung statement on why auto rotate was removed in One UI 6.1.1 was found.
- No formal review or study of Smart Stack rotation accuracy was found; the criticism above is inferred from how-to articles about turning it off.
- Apple's Watch user guide page for the Smart Stack could not be read (only its table of contents loaded), so the user steps for pinning and the stack's widget limit are not quoted here.

## 6. Accessibility: moving and resizing with VoiceOver, TalkBack, Switch Control, keyboard

### Takeaway
Both mobile platforms give screen-reader users a non-drag path built from discrete actions: Android's launcher exposes "Move item", then "Move to row R column C", and four resize actions with spoken confirmation; iOS exposes rotor actions to drag and to drop before or after another item. The iOS path was not extended when iOS 18 allowed free placement, which is a documented regression: an action vocabulary of "before/after" cannot express a position in a grid with gaps.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Android launcher, accessibility strings (AOSP Launcher3, `main` branch): hint "Double-tap & hold to move a widget or use custom actions."; actions "Move item", "Move item here", "Move to row %1$s column %2$s in %3$s" (third argument example: "Home screen 2 of 4"), "Move to position %1$s", "Move to favorites position %1$s", "Move to home screen", "Add to home screen"; confirmation "Item moved". Resize: action "Resize", then "Increase width", "Increase height", "Decrease width", "Decrease height"; confirmation "Widget resized to width %1$s height %2$s". Reconfigure button: "Change widget settings". — [AOSP Launcher3 strings.xml](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/apps/Launcher3/+/refs/heads/main/res/values/strings.xml)
- [S] iOS VoiceOver: since iOS 10 the Actions rotor offers moving apps; the sequence is turn on edit mode from the rotor, choose "Drag" on an item, move focus to another item, choose a drop option such as place before or place after; multi-item dragging since iOS 11. — AppleVis guide (archived), via search digest (the site refused a direct fetch, HTTP 403): [AppleVis, Dragging and dropping apps with VoiceOver](https://applevis.com/guides/dragging-dropping-apps-ios-1211-voiceover)
- [S] iOS 18 regression: with free placement, icons can go to any grid position, but the rotor's drop actions only allow placement before or after another icon; the reported workaround is the gesture path (double-tap and hold, then drag with one finger). Related reports: drops that unexpectedly create a folder, missing before/after options, the iPad Dock not accepting rotor drops; one folder bug is logged as fixed in iOS 18.4. — AppleVis bug tracker via search digest: [non-linear grid positions](https://applevis.com/bugs/ios/unable-move-home-screen-icons-non-linear-grid-positions-using-voiceover-actions-drag-drop), [rotor drag action inconsistent](https://www.applevis.com/bugs/ios/voiceover-rotor-drag-action-inconsistent-when-rearranging-home-screen-icons)
- [V] Apple's widget guidance on accessibility concerns content, not arrangement: "Avoid rasterizing text. Always use text elements and styles to ensure that your text scales well and to allow VoiceOver to speak your content."; widgets support Dynamic Type "from Large to AX5"; "Convey meaning without relying on specific colors". — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] On Mac and Windows the arrangement commands live in menus, which makes them keyboard-reachable by construction: macOS "Control-click a widget" for Edit, size, Remove; Windows three-dot menu for size, Customize, Remove; the board opens with Windows key + W. — [Apple Mac guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/add-and-customize-widgets-mchl52be5da5/mac); [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/stay-up-to-date-with-widgets-7ba79aaa-dac6-4687-b460-ad16a06be6e4)
- [V] Microsoft states contrast requirements for widget content with WCAG numbers (4.5:1 normal text, 3:1 large text and UI components). — [Microsoft Learn, Widget design fundamentals](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/widgets/widgets-design-fundamentals)

### Inferences
- Android's action set is the cleanest model for a keyboard and screen-reader path on a coordinate grid: pick up ("Move item"), choose among announced valid targets ("Move to row 2 column 3"), hear the result ("Item moved"); resize as four single-step actions with the resulting span spoken ("Widget resized to width 2 height 1"). It maps directly onto arrow keys plus a live region.
- The iOS 18 story is a warning about sequencing: the placement model changed from a sequence to coordinates, the assistive actions stayed sequence-shaped. If the stored arrangement is a sequence, "move before/after" suffices; if it is coordinates, the assistive path needs row and column targets.
- None of the consumer platforms documents a keyboard-only path for moving a widget by arrow keys; a web library has to meet WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.7 (dragging movements) on its own design. (Inference from absence in the sources read.)

### Gaps
- No Apple-authored documentation of VoiceOver or Switch Control steps for rearranging the Home Screen or resizing widgets was found; the iOS findings rest on community reports that could not be opened directly.
- Google's TalkBack help page for the Home screen covers navigation only; the custom actions are verified from launcher source, not from user documentation.
- Nothing was found on keyboard-only rearrangement of macOS desktop widgets or the Windows pinned section (for example arrow-key moves).
- Home Assistant's dashboard editor accessibility (keyboard drag of cards and sections) was not researched.

## 7. Home Assistant: Masonry, the 2024 Sections view, its evolution, community reaction, lessons on preserving spatial arrangement

### Takeaway
Home Assistant replaced a space-optimal auto-flow (Masonry) with a two-level model after concluding that auto-flow, multiple screen sizes and drag and drop cannot all hold at once: the user arranges cards inside a section on a fixed-column grid that never reflows, and the view reflows whole sections in reading order ("Z-Grid") by available width. The price is empty space where sections differ in height, and nearly every later addition (resizable cards, wide sections, dense placement, 12-column precise mode, heading cards) is a response to that price or to coarse card widths. Sections became the default in 2024.11.

### Cited Findings

**Masonry (the previous default)**

- [V] "The masonry view sorts cards in columns based on their card size." and "Masonry sorts cards in columns based on size and places the next card below the smallest card on the dashboard." Card size is an estimate the card returns: `getCardSize`, "A height of 1 is equivalent to 50 pixels." — [Home Assistant, Masonry view](https://www.home-assistant.io/dashboards/masonry/); [developer docs, Custom card](https://developers.home-assistant.io/docs/frontend/custom-ui/custom-card/)
- [V] The design team's diagnosis (Madelena, 4 Mar 2024): Masonry "lays out cards in a logic that may not be immediately clear and predictable to many users"; "Even a difference in height of 1 pixel would mean a card one would guess to be displayed on the leftmost column" lands elsewhere; "it is a wall of cards which does not care whether the bricks are laid" in a meaningful order; and "the muscle memory of where users remember the cards will be lost every time the dashboard is displayed on another screen." — [Home Assistant blog, A Home-Approved Dashboard chapter 1](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/03/04/dashboard-chapter-1/)
- [V] The central conclusion: "Masonry layout, compatibility with multiple screen sizes, and easy 'drag and drop' rearrangement of cards cannot co-exist." Also: "As it turns out, 'drag and drop' is not just an engineering problem; it is also a design problem." — [chapter 1](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/03/04/dashboard-chapter-1/)

**Sections view, release 2024.3 (March 2024, experimental)**

- [V] Unit of arrangement: "we are making these sections as the base unit of the view", because "our more advanced users are all naturally drawn to creating 'sections'" by hand with grid and markdown cards; "By grouping cards into 'sections', our users can reduce the number of items they need to scan through". — [chapter 1](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/03/04/dashboard-chapter-1/)
- [V] The reflow rule: "The view will rearrange the sections according to the amount of space available horizontally, while the number of columns of cards within each section stays the same". — [chapter 1](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/03/04/dashboard-chapter-1/)
- [V] The section order rule: "The Z-Grid works simply by laying out sections from left to right, and starting a new row when the row is full." Row height is set by the tallest section in the row (paraphrase). Chosen "due to its simplicity, predictability, and memorability as the default", "despite it may take up more space than other solutions." The post shows a comparison of four arrangement methods but names only the Z-Grid in text. — [chapter 1](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/03/04/dashboard-chapter-1/)
- [V] The card grid: "By introducing a grid system with cards of regular row height and column width multiples" cards land predictably; initially only Tile, Button and Sensor cards had standard sizes and "other cards will occupy the full width of a section by default at the moment". — [chapter 1](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/03/04/dashboard-chapter-1/)
- [V] Drag and drop: sections by a move handle, "Other sections will move out of the way for where the selected section will drop."; cards by "tap and hold anywhere on the card". — [chapter 1](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/03/04/dashboard-chapter-1/)
- [V] Method: the team "looked at dozens of different dashboards created by you", cites "months of user research and ideation", user interviews, and a standing User Testing Group. Authors: Madelena, with Matthias (matthiasdebaat) co-leading "Project Grace" and Paul (piitaya) on engineering. — [chapter 1](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/03/04/dashboard-chapter-1/)

**Evolution 2024.4 to 2024.11**

- [V] Timeline as the team lists it: max column option for sections (2024.4); drag and drop for card features (2024.5); visibility options for sections and cards, dashboard background (2024.6); resizable cards (2024.7); new badges (2024.8). — [Home Assistant blog, Dashboard chapter 2, 26 Jul 2024](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/07/26/dashboard-chapter-2/)
- [V] 2024.7, resizing: "Resizing is done with a very nice slider interface, found in the card editing menu under the new Layout tab", with a preview; "It works with every card"; "Most cards have been given a built-in minimum and maximum size, to make it easier to get your layouts just right."; a revert button restores the default. — [2024.7 release notes](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/07/03/release-20247/)
- [V] Grid density tuned after feedback: row height 66 px → 56 px, gutter 8 px, "We have reduced the height of each row on the grid to eliminate the empty space". — [chapter 2](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/07/26/dashboard-chapter-2/)
- [V] 2024.9, "Sections go BIG": "Sections can be expanded to span multiple sections."; "When switching to smaller screens, these larger sections will revert to one section wide." For cards that do not work narrow, "we would recommend using our device visibility settings." A card option to "always use the full width of the section". — [2024.9 release notes](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/09/04/release-20249/)
- [V] 2024.9, dense placement: "A dense section layout option will attempt to follow the Z-Grid rules, but will fill blank spaces whenever possible." Stated cost: "This can affect the order and predictability of your sections, but will maximize screen real estate." Docs: it "will remove some gaps, but it also means you have less control over the order of the cards" and "only applies to horizontal gaps if you used sections more than one column wide." — [2024.9 release notes](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/09/04/release-20249/); [Home Assistant, Sections view](https://www.home-assistant.io/dashboards/sections/)
- [V] 2024.10, heading card: section titles became "their own card, called the heading card"; "Making them a card rather than an inline title gives you a lot more flexibility."; subtitles "half the height"; headings can carry entity states and tap actions; existing titles migrated automatically. — [2024.10 release notes](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/10/02/release-202410/)
- [V] 2024.11, default and precise mode: Sections is "out of its experimental phase" and the default for new dashboards. "While a normal section is divided into 4 columns, precise modes gives you 12 columns", "which allows you to align 2, 3, 4, or 6 buttons side-by-side."; "Precise mode is applied on a card-by-card level." ### Inferences
- What Home Assistant learned about one arrangement across phone, tablet and wall display, in one sentence: spatial memory survives a change of width only if the thing that reflows is larger than the thing the user memorises. Cards keep their place inside a section at every width; sections keep their reading order and only wrap. On a phone the view is one section column, so a section looks the same as on a desktop.
- The cost was accepted knowingly ("despite it may take up more space") and it is the main thing users still complain about two years on. Each mitigation re-opens the original trade: dense placement regains space and gives up predictable order (the release notes say so); wide sections help large cards but "revert to one section wide" on small screens; power users end up with one view per display, which is the opposite of one arrangement everywhere.
- The card grid went from coarse to fine under use: 4 columns in March 2024, resizable in July, 12 columns ("precise mode") in November, with the coarse 4-step kept as the default interaction and multiples of 3 recommended. This is evidence that a coarse default with a fine escape hatch is where a user-arranged grid settles.
- Fixed row height (56 px + 8 px gap) with integer row spans is what makes drag and resize predictable; cards whose content does not fit the row grid are a continuing source of bug reports (tile features, vertical stacks). A data-dense library has to decide whether widget height is a span the user sets or a height the content dictates; Home Assistant allows both ("ignore the rows of the grid") and the mixed case is where gaps come from.
- Titles as ordinary cards (2024.10) rather than section chrome is a simplification worth noting: one kind of thing to move, resize and hide.
- The project's own next step after making arrangement easy was to stop requiring it: generated default dashboards (2025.9 experimental, default in 2026.2). Their roadmap's reading is that a blank slate intimidates. For a business library this argues for a shipped default arrangement that the user edits, not an empty canvas.

### Gaps
- The exact algorithm that maps viewport width to the number of section columns is not documented; the forum answer gives the CSS variable defaults (min 320 px, max 500 px, gap 32 px) but not the formula. `max_columns`, `dense_section_placement`, `column_span` and `row_span` are not described as YAML keys on the docs pages read.
- No published numbers from Home Assistant's user research (participants, task success) were found; the blog posts speak of "dozens" of dashboard case studies, interviews and a testing group only.
- The other three arrangement methods compared with the Z-Grid are shown as an image in chapter 1 and not named in the text; they could not be extracted.
- GitHub discussions in `home-assistant/frontend` asking for specific section features (sidebar inside a sections view, masonry-style section packing) were not located; the "most requested" picture above rests on forum threads and release notes.
- Whether a sections view has gained a native sidebar or masonry-style packing in 2025 or 2026 was not found in the sources read; the May 2026 forum reply says a masonry-style sections dashboard "isn't available".
- 2025.3, 2026.1 and 2026.2 release notes were not opened; their content above is from search digests.

## 8. Published rationale and user research from Apple, Google, Microsoft and Nabu Casa / Home Assistant

### Takeaway
Only Home Assistant published the reasoning behind its layout model, including the alternatives it rejected and the trade-off it accepted. Apple, Google and Microsoft publish principles and rules for widget authors; their statements about arrangement are announcements, and none of them publishes user research on placement.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Apple, principles (WWDC 2020, Mac, HI team): "Three useful principles we've found to focus on when thinking about ideas is to look for things that are personal, informational and contextual." — [WWDC 2020 session 10103](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10103/)
- [V] Apple, on widgets earning their place: "If a widget’s content never appears to change, people may not keep it in a prominent position."; "Replicating an app icon offers little additional value, and people may be less likely to keep it on their screens." — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] Apple, on not building apps inside widgets: "Offer simple, relevant functionality and reserve complexity for your app."; "avoid creating app-like layouts in your widgets." — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] Apple, on context-dependent rendering: a widget adapts "its appearance to the context in which they appear" with three rendering modes (full-colour, accented, vibrant) and, on Vision Pro, two distance thresholds: "When a person views the widget at a distance, display a simplified version of your widget that shows fewer details and has a larger type size, and remove interactive elements like buttons or toggles." — [Apple HIG, Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
- [V] Apple's only stated motive for free placement is expressive: placing items "right above the dock for easy access or perfectly framing a wallpaper". — [Apple Newsroom, 10 June 2024](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/06/ios-18-makes-iphone-more-personal-capable-and-intelligent-than-ever/)
- [S] Apple on the watchOS 10 redesign: Gary Butcher (Human Interface lead) tied it to larger, brighter displays and more sensors needing more than the original glanceable principles. — search digest: [Cool Hunting](https://coolhunting.com/tech/nerdy-details-from-the-redesign-of-apples-watchos)
- [V] Apple on glance duration (watch): "people should only be spending around ten seconds max engaging with glanceable information on their Watch." — [WWDC23 session 10309](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10309/)
- [V] Google: "Android 12 (API level 31) revamps the existing Widgets API to improve the user and developer experience in the platform and launchers." The one behavioural claim Google publishes about users: "One of the primary reasons users remove widgets is due to misalignment with other home screen elements." — [Android Developers, Android 12 widgets](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/12/features/widgets); [Android design guide, Widget sizing](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/widgets/sizing)
- [V] Google's getting-started list begins: "Choose a single primary use case to highlight in your widget." — [Android design guide, Widgets](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/widgets)
- [V] Microsoft's principles (page of 5 Jul 2026): *Glanceable* ("A widget should immediately deliver its primary value to the user."), *Focused & Useful* ("A widget should serve one clear, primary purpose."), *Predictable & Calm*, *User-Anchored* ("Content should be based on the user's own data or explicitly stated interests."), *Timely*. "Widgets are not meant to replace apps and websites". — [Microsoft Learn, Windows Widgets](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/widgets/)
- [V] Microsoft on density: "Aim for comfortable information density and healthy negative space within each widget size to help with the glance and go model."; "Avoid busy, complex layouts." — [Microsoft Learn, Widget states and built-in UI components](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/widgets/widgets-states-and-ui)
- [V] Home Assistant: the rationale in section 7 (chapter 1, chapter 2, the 2024 year-end roadmap), including the explicit impossibility statement about Masonry, screen sizes and drag and drop, the named trade-off of the Z-Grid, the goal of a "home-approved" dashboard for "everyone who lives in your home", and the method (dashboard case studies, interviews, a user testing group, an open design working group). — [chapter 1](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/03/04/dashboard-chapter-1/); [chapter 2](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/07/26/dashboard-chapter-2/); [roadmap](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2024/11/15/roadmap-2024h2/)

### Inferences
- The consumer guidelines are written for widgets that are glanced at for seconds ("glance and go", "ten seconds max"). A data-dense business dashboard is read longer and worked in. The size and gallery mechanics transfer; the density advice (generous negative space, one to four touch targets) does not transfer unchanged, and Apple's own density sentence cuts both ways ("Sparse layouts can make the widget seem unnecessary").
- Home Assistant is the closest model to the library's case: a web application, cards of real density, one arrangement shown on phone, tablet and wall display, edited by end users. It is also the only source with a published argument for its layout model and a public record of what users asked for afterwards.
- The three OS vendors agree on a division of labour that a library can adopt as its contract: the host owns placement, size switching, chrome, error and loading states and the gallery; the widget owns content per declared size, a default configuration and an optional configuration surface.

### Gaps
- No Nielsen Norman Group article or other formal usability study of widget arrangement (iOS 14, iOS 18, Android) was found in this session; reviewer reaction comes from MacStories (read) and from Tom's Guide, Digital Trends and forum posts (search digests only).
- No published user research from Apple, Google or Microsoft on placement models, resize handles or stacks was found.
- Material Design's own widget guidance (m3.material.io) was not consulted; the Android design guide on developer.android.com was used instead.
