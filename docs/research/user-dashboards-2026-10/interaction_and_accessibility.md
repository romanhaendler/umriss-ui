## 1. WCAG 2.2: what the success criteria require of a rearrangeable grid; visual order versus DOM order; CSS `reading-flow`

### Takeaway
Two criteria bind independently: 2.1.1 demands a keyboard route for moving and resizing, 2.5.7 demands a single-pointer route without dragging, and the Understanding document says outright that the first does not satisfy the second. A control that is both clickable and focusable (buttons, a menu) satisfies both at once. Visual order that departs from DOM order is not an automatic failure of 1.3.2 or 2.4.3, but "DOM order matches visual order" (C27) is the listed sufficient technique, and CSS `reading-flow` is no remedy in 2026: Chromium only since version 137, no Firefox, no Safari, and WebKit on record as "not ready to support the proposal as-is".

### Cited Findings

**2.5.7 Dragging Movements (AA)**
- Normative text: "All functionality that uses a dragging movement for operation can be achieved by a single pointer without dragging, unless dragging is essential or the functionality is determined by the user agent and not modified by the author." — [Understanding SC 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)
- Definition of dragging movement: "an operation where the pointer engages with an element on the down-event and the element (or a representation of its position) follows the pointer until an up-event". — [Understanding SC 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)
- Keyboard support is not enough: "This requirement is separate from keyboard accessibility because people using a touchscreen device ..." and "achieving keyboard equivalence for a dragging operation does not automatically meet this success criterion"; the two criteria are evaluated independently. — [Understanding SC 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)
- The alternative need not sit on the dragged element: it "does not have to be the same component, so long as the functionality is equivalent." The page sets no limit on the number of steps; several of its examples take two or more clicks. — [Understanding SC 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)
- The single-pointer alternative must not itself be a path-based gesture such as a swipe (that would fall under 2.5.1). — [Understanding SC 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)
- Examples of acceptable alternatives given by the Understanding page (ten in all), those relevant to a grid: a sortable list where, after selecting an element, "adjacent controls for moving the element up or down" appear; a task board where items are moved "by tapping or clicking on pop-up menu entries"; a kanban board where an item is selected and moved by "activating an arrow button to move the selected element"; a selection rectangle drawn by a mode in which the user can "click/tap the first corner, then click/tap the opposite corner"; a map with up/down/left/right buttons. — [Understanding SC 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)
- Sufficient technique G219, examples: (1) sortable list - after a single pointer activation "the list items display up and down arrows"; (2) priority list - each item has a number field and "For any of the items, users can put in another number"; (3) kanban - select items, then a drop-down labelled "Move selected items to"; (4) radial control - click another position. Failure F108: "not providing a single pointer method". — [G219](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G219), [Understanding SC 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)
- Scope: the criterion does not apply to scrolling and dragging "enabled by the user agent" (scrollbars, touch scrolling, pull to refresh), but it does apply where content suppresses the browser's scrolling or implements its own. — [Understanding SC 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)
- Neither the Understanding page nor G219 mentions a grid. — [Understanding SC 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html), [G219](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G219)

**2.1.1 Keyboard (A)**
- Normative text: "All functionality of the content is operable through a keyboard interface without requiring specific timings for individual keystrokes, except where the underlying function requires input that depends on the path of the user's movement and not just the endpoints." Note 1: "This exception relates to the underlying function, not the input technique." — [Understanding SC 2.1.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html)
- Moving and resizing are explicitly not path-dependent: "Drawing straight lines, regular geometric shapes, re-sizing windows and dragging objects to a location (when the path to that location is not relevant) do not require path dependent input." — [Understanding SC 2.1.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html)
- Example 2: "An application that uses drag and drop also supports "cut" and "paste" or form controls to move objects." — [Understanding SC 2.1.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html)

**2.5.1 Pointer Gestures (A)**
- Normative text: "All functionality that uses multipoint or path-based gestures for operation can be operated with a single pointer without a path-based gesture, unless a multipoint or path-based gesture is essential." — [Understanding SC 2.5.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-gestures.html)
- Free drag and drop is not a path-based gesture: dragging that "does not require the user to follow any particular path, direction, or speed, is therefore not a path-based gesture"; 2.5.1 "does not apply to gestures that involve dragging in any direction"; "These cases are covered in Success Criterion 2.5.7 Dragging Movements." — [Understanding SC 2.5.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-gestures.html)
- What 2.5.1 does bind in a dashboard: pinch-to-resize or two-finger gestures need a single-pointer alternative, and that alternative "can't exclusively rely on a dragging movement, as this would fail the requirements of 2.5.7". — [Understanding SC 2.5.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-gestures.html)

**2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum) (AA)**
- Normative text: "The size of the target for pointer inputs is at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels, except when:" Spacing - "Undersized targets (those less than 24 by 24 CSS pixels) are positioned so that if a 24 CSS pixel diameter circle is centered on the bounding box of each, the circles do not intersect another target or the circle for another undersized target;" Equivalent - "The function can be achieved through a different control on the same page that meets this criterion;" Inline; User Agent Control; Essential. — [Understanding SC 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)
- The page does not discuss drag handles or resize handles. — [Understanding SC 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)

**2.5.2 Pointer Cancellation (A)** (not in the brief, but it governs the drop)
- "Completion of the function is on the up-event, and a mechanism is available to abort the function before completion or to undo the function after completion;" - for drag and drop the page says the user can abort by returning the item or releasing where a move is disallowed ("Releasing the pointer outside the drop target area reverts the action"), or the design may offer a confirmation or an undo button after the drop. — [Understanding SC 2.5.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-cancellation.html)

**1.3.2 Meaningful Sequence (A)**
- Normative text: "When the sequence in which content is presented affects its meaning, a correct reading sequence can be programmatically determined." — [Understanding SC 1.3.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/meaningful-sequence.html)
- "A sequence is meaningful if the order of content in the sequence cannot be changed without affecting its meaning." "The order of content in a sequence is not always meaningful." "Providing a particular linear order is only required where it affects meaning". Unordered lists are named as not meaningful sequences; ordered lists and tables are. — [Understanding SC 1.3.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/meaningful-sequence.html)
- Example 2 passes a page whose navigation, main story and side story are positioned by CSS so that "The visual presentation of the sections does not match the programmatically determined order", because meaning does not depend on that order. Failure F1: "Changing the meaning of content by positioning information with CSS". Sufficient technique C27: "Making the DOM order match the visual order." The page does not mention CSS grid, flexbox or the `order` property. — [Understanding SC 1.3.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/meaningful-sequence.html)

**2.4.3 Focus Order (A)**
- Normative text: "If a web page can be navigated sequentially and the navigation sequences affect meaning or operation, focusable components receive focus in an order that preserves meaning and operability." — [Understanding SC 2.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-order.html)
- "Focus order does not necessarily need to follow the visual layout of the web page, as long as the order in which elements receive focus is logical, [and] the hierarchy and relationship of content implied by the visual presentation is preserved." "As a best practice, though, make sure the focus order reinforces the reading order implied by the visual layout." "If there is more than one order that preserves meaning and operability, only one of them needs to be provided." — [Understanding SC 2.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-order.html)
- Two independent columns: "it is not a failure if elements in the right-hand column receive focus first, followed by the elements in the left-hand column." On CSS: "it is not necessarily true that all CSS positioning would" pass; "More complex positioning examples may or may not preserve meaning and operability." Failures F44 (tabindex) and F85 (dialogs or menus not adjacent to their trigger in the sequential order). — [Understanding SC 2.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-order.html)

**4.1.3 Status Messages (AA)**
- Normative text: "In content implemented using markup languages, status messages can be programmatically determined through role or properties such that they can be presented to the user by assistive technologies without receiving focus." A status message is a "change in content that is not a change of context" that "provides information to the user on the success or results of an action" (and on state, progress or errors). — [Understanding SC 4.1.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html)
- Techniques: ARIA22 `role=status` for results of an action; ARIA19 `role=alert` for errors; ARIA23 `role=log` for sequential updates. Examples are of the form "5 items" after adding to a cart or "Saved in 'Wedding' album". Changes of context take focus and are outside the criterion. — [Understanding SC 4.1.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html)

**CSS `reading-flow` / `reading-order` in 2026**
- `reading-flow` sets the order in which children of a block, flex or grid container "are rendered to speech and navigated to when using sequential navigation"; values `normal`, `flex-visual`, `flex-flow`, `grid-rows` (visual order row by row), `grid-columns`, `grid-order` (DOM order modified by `order`), `source-order` (keeps DOM order but lets children set `reading-order`, an integer, default 0). Positive `tabindex` on direct children of a reading-flow container is ignored. MDN marks it experimental, "Limited availability", not Baseline, and still recommends source order that matches the layout where possible. — [MDN reading-flow](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/reading-flow)
- Chrome: "available from Chrome 137"; article dated 1 May 2025; it changes Tab order and the order exposed to accessibility tools; a reading-flow container becomes a focus scope owner. The article makes no statement about other browsers and does not say how a screen reader's browse mode behaves. — [Chrome for Developers, "Use CSS reading-flow for logical sequential focus navigation"](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/reading-flow)
- Support table: Chrome and Edge from 137 (including Chrome for Android); Firefox not supported in any listed version; Safari and iOS Safari not supported through 26.x. *(search snippet of the caniuse table)* — [caniuse: reading-flow](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_properties_reading-flow)
- WebKit: standards-position request #378, opened 31 July 2024, carries no support/neutral/oppose label. A comment of 10 December 2025 (fantasai) states concerns about interactions with accessibility, tab navigation, text selection and caret navigation and about developer ergonomics; WebKit is "not ready to support the proposal as-is" though not "opposed fundamentally". On 24 June 2026 Rachel Andrew asked again for details; no WebKit reply in the thread. — [WebKit/standards-positions #378](https://github.com/WebKit/standards-positions/issues/378)
- Adrian Roselli's position on the underlying problem (2019): a screen reader presents the page in source order, so a layout that visually shuffles it can fail 1.3.2; non-interactive content is not reached by Tab, which makes reading-order faults harder to spot. *(search snippet)* — [Adrian Roselli, Reading Order Bookmarklet](https://adrianroselli.com/2019/04/reading-order-bookmarklet.html)

### Inferences
- Moving a widget and resizing a widget each need three routes: pointer drag (optional), single pointer without drag (2.5.7), keyboard (2.1.1). A button or menu on the widget that offers "move ..." and "size ..." actions is one implementation that covers the second and third route together; keyboard-only "pick up and move with arrows" covers only 2.1.1 and leaves 2.5.7 open. Vendors do blur this: AG Studio 3.0 claims 2.5.7 support "through keyboard equivalents for drag actions" ([AG Studio accessibility](https://www.ag-grid.com/studio/react/accessibility/)), which the Understanding text contradicts.
- Whether out-of-DOM-order placement fails depends on whether the order carries meaning or affects operation. Dashboard widgets are mostly independent of one another, like the two independent columns of 2.4.3, so a divergence is not automatically a failure. But on a dashboard whose arrangement the reader chose, the arrangement is the content the reader authored; a Tab sequence or a screen reader's reading that jumps around the grid makes "where did my widget go after the move" unanswerable, which is an operability problem. The conservative rule is C27: render the widgets in DOM order equal to the arrangement's sequence.
- Consequence for layout: `grid-auto-flow: dense` and the `order` property both produce visual orders that differ from DOM order. Dense packing is only safe if the library computes the packed order itself and writes the DOM in that order (so the packed result is the sequence), or if gaps are left open. `reading-flow: grid-rows` would repair dense packing in Chromium, but with no Firefox and no Safari support it can be at most a progressive enhancement in 2026, not the mechanism conformance rests on.
- Handles: a drag handle and a resize handle are pointer targets and should each be at least 24 by 24 CSS px, or pass the spacing exception. The "Equivalent" exception would formally let a small corner grip pass if the same function is available through a conforming control (for example a size menu), but that is a loophole, not a design goal.
- After a move, resize, add or remove, the outcome should be exposed through `role=status` (4.1.3) rather than by moving focus.

- Mozilla's standards position on `reading-flow` was not retrieved (a W3C list message refers to mozilla/standards-positions issue #1056; its content was not seen). Firefox "not supported" rests on the caniuse snippet only.
- MDN's compatibility table itself did not come through; version numbers rest on the Chrome article and the caniuse snippet.

## 2. Established accessible drag-and-drop patterns, and what they recommend for a 2D grid

### Takeaway
There are two schools. One emulates the drag on the keyboard (Salesforce 2017, dnd-kit, React Aria, Cloudscape Board, GitLab's proposal: pick up, arrows or Tab, drop, Escape). The other replaces the drag with explicit outcomes in a menu or dialog (Atlassian's Pragmatic drag and drop, GitHub's "move dialog", every WCAG example). The second school's argument has the better evidence in 2024-2026: GitHub's user testing found that many people preferred the dialog, and only explicit actions satisfy 2.5.7 as well. No source prescribes a pattern for a 2D grid; the sources that touch 2D split by data model, sequence versus coordinates.

### Cited Findings

**W3C ARIA**
- `aria-grabbed` and `aria-dropeffect` are deprecated in ARIA 1.1; a 2015 working-group thread shows the decision (Léonie Watson: "It's a bad API that should be culled from the 1.1 spec"; Richard Schwerdtfeger opposed removal because HTML had delivered no replacement). *(search snippets; the ARIA 1.2 section itself did not load)* — [public-pfwg, Sept 2015](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-pfwg/2015Sep/0187.html), [digitala11y on aria-grabbed](https://www.digitala11y.com/?p=1818)
- ARIA 1.2 on deprecated features in general: they "are allowed in the conformance model and expected to be supported by user agents, but it is recommended that authors do not use it for new content." — [WAI-ARIA 1.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/wai-aria-1.2/)

**Salesforce, "4 Major Patterns for Accessible Drag and Drop" (Jesse Hausler, Salesforce UX, November 2017)**
- The article argues for making the interaction itself keyboard-accessible instead of substituting a form; it frames every step as identity, operation and state conveyed through native controls, WAI-ARIA and live regions, and says live regions suit status updates because they "jump text to the front of 'the queue' of a screen reader". Patterns include sorting a list, moving items between lists, and placing and sizing an object on a canvas. *(search snippets only; the article returned HTTP 403)* — [Salesforce UX on Medium](https://medium.com/salesforce-ux/4-major-patterns-for-accessible-drag-and-drop-1d43f64ebf09)
- The companion repository `salesforce-ux/dnd-a11y-patterns` credits Jesse Hausler's article, uses React and SLDS styles, and has been archived and read-only since 9 June 2025. — [GitHub: salesforce-ux/dnd-a11y-patterns](https://github.com/salesforce-ux/dnd-a11y-patterns)
- NN/g describes the Salesforce model as its accessibility recommendation: handle reachable with Tab, spacebar grabs, screen-reader messages describe the available actions "(typically arrow keys to move or resize)", whether the item is grabbed, and its current position or size. — [NN/g, Drag-and-Drop: How to Design for Ease of Use](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/drag-drop/)
- A 2024 WebAIM list thread reports the Salesforce example "works well with the keyboard, but not with the screen reader on", because the screen reader does not switch to focus mode by itself. *(search snippet)* — [WebAIM list, message 50453](https://webaim.org/discussion/mail_message?id=50453)

**Atlassian, Pragmatic drag and drop - accessibility guidelines**
- "Always provide alternatives to dragging"; "Not all people can successfully complete pointer based drag operations." Alternative flows have three steps: let the user trigger an outcome, tell the user what they achieved, let them continue. — [Atlassian Design: accessibility guidelines](https://atlassian.design/components/pragmatic-drag-and-drop/accessibility-guidelines)
- Against directional (arrow-key) controls for most experiences: directional movement "doesn't always make sense when you can't see the interface"; it requires JAWS users to change screen reader mode; it "does not translate well to all experiences"; "Complex operations can require many keystrokes"; it "takes a significant amount of code to get working". The core package enables no accessible controls by default "as there is no one pattern that works well for all situations." — [Atlassian Design: accessibility guidelines](https://atlassian.design/components/pragmatic-drag-and-drop/accessibility-guidelines)
- Recommended controls: a "More" (...) button per draggable entity that opens a menu of outcomes (for example "move to top"); if moving is the only action, the drag handle itself becomes a menu button; one trigger button per entity; outcomes that need more input "trigger a modal containing a form". — [Atlassian Design: accessibility guidelines](https://atlassian.design/components/pragmatic-drag-and-drop/accessibility-guidelines)
- Announcements: live regions "should provide real-time feedback both during and after an interaction" and include "the name of the item being moved, as well as its old and new position", for example 'Task "Clean dishes" moved to list "Doing" from "Todo".' Accessible names of buttons "include the name of the element being acted on". — [Atlassian Design: accessibility guidelines](https://atlassian.design/components/pragmatic-drag-and-drop/accessibility-guidelines)
- Focus: return focus "to the original trigger element whenever possible"; when an element is moved to a new parent it remounts and focus must be restored by hand. The page does not mention grids. — [Atlassian Design: accessibility guidelines](https://atlassian.design/components/pragmatic-drag-and-drop/accessibility-guidelines)

**GitHub, "Exploring the challenges in creating an accessible sortable list (drag-and-drop)" (Kendall Gassner, 9 July 2024)**
- GitHub's accessibility team calls drag and drop a "high-risk pattern" and scoped its work to "one-dimensional drag-and-drop". — [GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/engineering/user-experience/exploring-the-challenges-in-creating-an-accessible-sortable-list-drag-and-drop/)
- Arrow keys collide with screen-reader navigation; their fix was `role='application'` on the drag trigger, added only during an active drag and removed on completion or cancel. NVDA fires mouse events on Enter/Space, which could end a drag falsely; they separated a visible icon button for the keyboard from an invisible overlay for the mouse. — [GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/engineering/user-experience/exploring-the-challenges-in-creating-an-accessible-sortable-list-drag-and-drop/)
- Announcements went stale when users moved items quickly, a fault their own slow testing missed and user testing exposed; fix: 100 ms debounce and `aria-live='assertive'` so the newest position interrupts older ones. Many users had never used keyboard drag and drop; fix: an instruction dialog on keyboard activation with "don't show this again". — [GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/engineering/user-experience/exploring-the-challenges-in-creating-an-accessible-sortable-list-drag-and-drop/)
- Voice-control users could drop unintentionally when the list scrolled during a drag; GitHub added a second mechanism, a **move dialog** with an action field (move before/after), a position field and a live preview announced via `aria-live`. "Many users who don't use voice control also preferred it, reporting greater confidence." Recommendation: support more than one interaction mode; test with real users working at realistic speed. — [GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/engineering/user-experience/exploring-the-challenges-in-creating-an-accessible-sortable-list-drag-and-drop/)

**dnd-kit**
- Keyboard sensor defaults: Space or Enter picks up, arrow keys move (25 px per press by default), Space or Enter drops, Escape cancels. Default instructions: "To pick up a draggable item, press space or enter. While dragging, use the arrow keys to move the item in any given direction. Press space or enter again to drop the item in its new position, or press escape to cancel." — [dnd-kit accessibility guide](https://dndkit.com/guides/accessibility)
- Default announcements: "Picked up draggable item ${active.id}." / "Draggable item ${active.id} was moved over droppable area ${over.id}." / "Draggable item ${active.id} was dropped over droppable area ${over.id}" / "Dragging was cancelled. Draggable item ${active.id} was dropped." The guide tells authors to replace these with position-based wording ("Item at position 1") instead of ids. — [dnd-kit accessibility guide](https://dndkit.com/guides/accessibility)
- Attributes: `role="button"` (a real `<button>` preferred), `aria-roledescription="draggable"` (a more specific value recommended), `aria-describedby` pointing at the instructions, `tabindex=0`. The sortable preset replaces free 25 px movement with a coordinate getter that finds the next sortable element in the pressed direction. — [dnd-kit accessibility guide](https://dndkit.com/guides/accessibility)

**React Aria / React Spectrum (Adobe)**
- Keyboard: Enter on a draggable starts a page-wide drag mode; Tab cycles only through drop targets that accept the data; Enter drops; arrow keys choose a drop position inside a collection. Drop positions are "root", "on" an item and "between" items, the last through indicators labelled like "Insert between item A and item B". — [React Aria blog: drag and drop](https://react-aria.adobe.com/blog/drag-and-drop)
- Screen readers: during the drag everything except valid drop targets is hidden from assistive technology; touch screen-reader users swipe between targets and double-tap to drop; a live region announces start (with instructions) and whether the drop succeeded or was cancelled; prompts adapt to the device and are localised into over 30 languages. — [React Aria blog: drag and drop](https://react-aria.adobe.com/blog/drag-and-drop)

**Cloudscape (AWS) - Board and drag-and-drop pattern**
- Pattern: offer "single pointer and keyboard alternatives so users can complete moves without dragging"; for mouse or trackpad "use two pointer events within the drag handle" and "Add directional arrow icon buttons for single-click moves" - a click on the handle followed by clicks on direction buttons. Handles: `drag-indicator` for moving, `resize-area` (cursor `nwse-resize`), `horizontal-resize`, `vertical-resize`. — [Cloudscape: drag-and-drop pattern](https://cloudscape.design/patterns/general/drag-and-drop/)
- The Board component's string interface has live announcements for the start of an operation (reorder, resize or insert), for each reorder, resize and insert step, for commit, for discard and for removal of an item. The component leaves loading and error states and their aria-live announcements to the client. — [cloudscape-design/board-components, interfaces.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cloudscape-design/board-components/main/src/board/interfaces.ts)

**GitLab Pajamas - keyboard support for the dashboard grid (issue, 2025)**
- Gridstack "doesn't support keyboard-only (and therefore screen reader driven) editing". Proposal: Tab to a panel or resize handle, Space to select, arrow keys to move or resize, Space to confirm, `aria-live` for position and size, `aria-describedby` for instructions. Stated drawback: after items move, the native tab order can become unintuitive. References Salesforce's article and dragon-drop. — [GitLab design.gitlab.com issue 2885](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-org/gitlab-services/design.gitlab.com/-/issues/2885)

**Vispero/TPGi, "The Road to Accessible Drag and Drop (Part 1)" (James Edwards, 19 December 2023)**
- Requirements list: "All functionality is operable with single pointer actions, without the need for dragging movements"; full keyboard, mouse and touch support; support for virtual pointers (voice control, eye tracking); keyboard interaction intuitive and guessable, with instructions. State exposed through attributes, not only described in text. "Live regions are not entirely reliable" - avoid them where an alternative exists. "each draggable item should be a single interactive control", without inner buttons or handles. Relevant criteria it lists: 1.3.1, 2.1.1, 2.3.3, 2.5.2, 2.5.3, 2.5.6, 2.5.7, 2.5.8, 3.3.2, 4.1.2. — [Vispero: The Road to Accessible Drag and Drop, Part 1](https://vispero.com/resources/the-road-to-accessible-drag-and-drop-part-1/)

**Products with a documented keyboard move in two dimensions**
- Windows 10 and 11 Start: "Alt + Shift + arrow keys" moves the focused tile or group in the arrow's direction; "Ctrl + Shift + arrow keys" moves a tile onto another to make a folder. — [Microsoft Support: Keyboard shortcuts in Windows](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/keyboard-shortcuts-in-windows-dcc61a57-8ff0-cffe-9796-cb9706c75eec)
- Power BI Desktop (in editing mode): arrow keys move a visual in small steps, Shift + arrow keys in bigger steps. — [Microsoft Learn: Keyboard shortcuts in Power BI Desktop](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-bi/create-reports/desktop-accessibility-keyboard-shortcuts)
- AG Studio 3.0 (dashboard builder): every drag-based action "has a keyboard equivalent"; changes announced through a polite live region "including the resulting position of a moved item"; tested with JAWS and VoiceOver; the vendor calls it "primarily a mouse-driven application". — [AG Studio accessibility](https://www.ag-grid.com/studio/react/accessibility/)
- Grafana (docs for the current version, apparently 13.1): keyboard shortcuts exist for a hovered panel - edit (`e`), full screen (`v`), duplicate (`p d`), remove (`p r`) - but none for moving or resizing a panel. — [Grafana docs: use dashboards](https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/latest/dashboards/use-dashboards/)

### Inferences
- Mapping the sources onto the four candidate models for a 2D grid:
  - *Arrows move by cell* (Power BI, Windows Start, Salesforce's canvas pattern, GitLab's proposal, dnd-kit's default): natural where the arrangement is stored as coordinates and gaps are allowed. Weak for screen-reader users (Atlassian's objection; GitHub's `role=application` workaround; the WebAIM report on Salesforce's demo) and unpredictable where other widgets are pushed or packed after each step.
  - *Arrows move in sequence* (dnd-kit sortable, GitHub's move mode): natural where the arrangement is a sequence; Left/Up = earlier, Right/Down = later is easy to announce as "position 3 of 8". In a wrapping grid, Up/Down as "one row" is ambiguous when widgets differ in size, so mapping all four arrows onto earlier/later is the simpler contract.
  - *Pick up / move / drop mode with Space or Enter and Escape*: the common envelope around either arrow model (Salesforce, dnd-kit, React Aria, Cloudscape, GitLab). It provides cancel for free and one announcement on commit, at the cost of a mode that must be explained (GitHub needed an instruction dialog) and of live-region traffic during the move.
  - *Menu with "move earlier / later / to position"* (Atlassian, GitHub's dialog, WCAG's own examples, G219's number field): the only model that satisfies 2.5.7 and 2.1.1 together, works with voice control, switch access and touch screen readers without special modes, and needs one announcement per action. It is slower for long moves unless "move to position N" or "move to start/end" is offered.
- For a library whose glossary already defines the arrangement as a sequence, the best-supported combination is: (a) a per-widget actions menu on a real button with move earlier, move later, move to start, move to end (or to position), the sizes, and remove - this is the conformance baseline; (b) optional keyboard shortcuts on the focused widget or its handle as an accelerator for sighted keyboard users (the Windows and Power BI precedent), which change the sequence by one step per press; (c) pointer drag as the third route. A modal "fake drag" is then unnecessary.
- What to announce, consolidated from Atlassian, dnd-kit, GitHub, AG Studio and 4.1.3: after each completed action, one message with the widget's name, the action and the resulting position ("Revenue moved to position 3 of 8"; "Revenue is now wide"; "Revenue removed. Undo available."), in a `role=status` (polite) region. Use assertive only inside a live move mode, where a stale position is worse than an interruption, and debounce by about 100 ms. Never announce ids. Instructions belong in `aria-describedby` on the control, not in the live region.
- Focus after an action should stay on the control that triggered it (Atlassian). If the widget's DOM node is re-created by the reorder, focus must be restored explicitly; keeping React keys stable avoids the remount for moves within one container.
- Vispero's "single interactive control per draggable" and Atlassian's "one trigger button per entity" both argue against a separate focusable drag handle plus a separate menu button plus a separate resize grip per widget: three tab stops per widget times many widgets is a long tab sequence. One actions button per widget keeps it to one.

### Gaps
- Salesforce's article text was not readable in this run; the exact keys, announcement strings, and the keyboard behaviour of its canvas resize pattern are unverified here. Commonly reported (NN/g, GitLab, practitioners) is Space to grab, arrows to move or resize, Space to drop, Escape to cancel.
- React Aria's blog, as fetched, names no cancel key and no publication date and does not mention `aria-grabbed`/`aria-dropeffect`; its stated reason for Tab over arrows is only that Tab limits focus to valid targets.
- Whether the W3C ARIA Authoring Practices Guide has a drag-and-drop pattern was not checked against the APG site in this run; no APG drag-and-drop page surfaced in any search.
- Cloudscape Board's keys and the wording of its announcements were not verified (the component page renders by script); only the announcement hooks are confirmed.
- Shopify Polaris and Adobe Spectrum's own design guidance on drag and drop were not researched; React Spectrum is covered through React Aria.
- Parts 2 and 3 of the Vispero series, which contain the implementation, were not read.
- No source gives tested guidance for screen-reader users moving an item in a grid with items of different sizes.

## 3. Keyboard resize: patterns and examples in real products

### Takeaway
Resizing is treated by WCAG exactly like moving: not path-dependent, so keyboard operable (2.1.1), and needing a non-drag pointer route (2.5.7). Documented practice falls into two patterns - a focusable resize handle operated with arrow keys (Power BI, Salesforce's canvas pattern, GitLab's proposal, Cloudscape) and a discrete choice of sizes - and documentation of either in shipping dashboard products is thin; Grafana documents none.

### Cited Findings
- WCAG's Intent for 2.1.1 lists "re-sizing windows" among functions that "do not require path dependent input". — [Understanding SC 2.1.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html)
- Power BI Desktop, in editing mode: "Resize a visual: Arrow keys" (with focus on a resize handle), next to "Move a visual on the canvas (small moves): Arrow keys" and "(bigger moves): Shift + arrow keys"; "Maintain aspect ratio: Shift + drag the resize handles". Page dated 1 December 2025, updated 23 July 2026. — [Microsoft Learn: Keyboard shortcuts in Power BI Desktop](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-bi/create-reports/desktop-accessibility-keyboard-shortcuts)
- Windows 10: "Ctrl + arrow keys: Resize the Start menu when it's open." — [Microsoft Support: Keyboard shortcuts in Windows](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/keyboard-shortcuts-in-windows-dcc61a57-8ff0-cffe-9796-cb9706c75eec)
- GitLab's proposal for its Gridstack-based dashboard: the resize handle is its own focus stop; Space selects it, arrow keys resize, Space confirms; size changes are announced through `aria-live`. — [GitLab design.gitlab.com issue 2885](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-org/gitlab-services/design.gitlab.com/-/issues/2885)
- Cloudscape Board: resize is one of three operation types with its own start, step, commit and discard announcements; sizes are whole columns and rows (`columnSpan` from 1, `rowSpan` from 2), each item declares `minColumnSpan`/`minRowSpan` and a default size taken on insertion. — [cloudscape-design/board-components, interfaces.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cloudscape-design/board-components/main/src/board/interfaces.ts)
- NN/g: screen-reader messages for an accessible handle should state the available actions "(typically arrow keys to move or resize)" and the item's "current position or size"; a resize signifier must say that dragging will resize and not move (one-dimensional crossbar cursor for a divider, diagonal arrow for two-dimensional resize). — [NN/g, Drag-and-Drop: How to Design for Ease of Use](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/drag-drop/)
- Grafana: no keyboard or menu route for resizing a panel is documented. — [Grafana docs: use dashboards](https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/latest/dashboards/use-dashboards/)
- Wijmo 2026 v1 added keyboard shortcuts for dragging and resizing columns and rows, an indication that grid vendors are closing this gap in 2026. *(search snippet)* — [ComponentSource: Wijmo 2026 v1](https://www.componentsource.com/pt-br/product/wijmo/releases/2958676)
- The Salesforce article rejects a form field as a substitute for keyboard resize of table columns. *(search snippet)* — [Salesforce UX on Medium](https://medium.com/salesforce-ux/4-major-patterns-for-accessible-drag-and-drop-1d43f64ebf09)

### Inferences
- Where a widget's size is a small set of named sizes (the common case for end-user dashboards: small/medium/large, or column spans 1 to n), a menu or radio group of sizes is the simplest conforming control: one activation, one announcement ("Revenue is now wide, 2 of 4 columns"), no mode, valid for pointer, keyboard, voice and switch alike. A corner grip dragged by pointer can then snap to the same set.
- Where sizes are free spans in two dimensions, the handle-with-arrows pattern needs a mode (select, arrows, confirm, Escape) and announcements per step; "wider/narrower/taller/shorter" buttons or menu items give the same reach without a mode and also satisfy 2.5.7.
- Shift+Arrow as "resize" on the widget itself conflicts with Power BI's precedent (Shift+Arrow = bigger move) and with text selection habits; no product surveyed uses Shift+Arrow for resize. If shortcuts are offered, they need to be discoverable from the menu (shortcut hints) because no convention exists.

### Gaps
- Azure Portal, Datadog, Salesforce Lightning App Builder and the Windows 11 widgets board: no official documentation of keyboard resize was found. Azure's tile context menu with fixed sizes and Lightning App Builder's keyboard model are widely described but were not verified against a primary source in this run (Salesforce's Trailhead screen-reader instructions returned HTTP 403).
- iOS/iPadOS widget resizing (handle in edit mode) could not be verified: Apple's support page did not load its body.
- No usability data comparing a size menu against a keyboard-operated handle was found.

## 4. Pointer feel: reflow, previews, handles, activation, cancel

### Takeaway
The guidance that exists is consistent on feedback at every stage (signifier, grab, preview, drop, failure) and on a few numbers: reshuffle when the dragged item's centre crosses a neighbour's edge, animate in about 100 ms, show the drag image after about 3 points of movement, keep handles at 24 px or more. On the central choice - neighbours moving out of the way versus a stable insertion indicator - the authorities disagree: NN/g recommends live "make room" for lists, Atlassian's current system uses a line and does not move anything. Dragging is measurably slower and three to four times more error-prone than pointing.

### Cited Findings
- Dragging versus pointing (MacKenzie, Sellen, Buxton, CHI '91, 12 participants): mouse movement time 674 ms pointing versus 916 ms dragging, error rate 3.5% versus 10.8%; tablet 665/802 ms and 4.0%/13.6%; trackball 1101/1284 ms and 3.9%/17.3%. "For State 2 tasks, movement times are longer and error rates are higher." — [MacKenzie, Sellen, Buxton 1991](https://www.yorku.ca/mack/CHI91.html)
- NN/g (Page Laubheimer, 23 February 2020): "Clear signifiers and clear feedback at all stages of the interaction make drag-and-drop discoverable and easy to use." Drag and drop can be "inefficient, imprecise, and physically tiring"; it often causes errors such as dropping in the wrong spot. — [NN/g, Drag-and-Drop: How to Design for Ease of Use](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/drag-drop/)
- NN/g on reorder preview: show other items moving out of the way before release; animate in roughly 100 ms with easing instead of redrawing instantly; trigger the reshuffle "when the center of the dragged object crosses the edge of the underlying object" - triggering on the dragged object's edge feels twitchy, triggering on the cursor position feels mushy. — [NN/g, Drag-and-Drop: How to Design for Ease of Use](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/drag-drop/)
- NN/g on magnetism: make the drop zone active slightly beyond its visible border and show that it is active (dotted border, highlight, animated preview of where the item will snap). On precision: pair drag with a precise method, for example arrow keys to fine-tune. — [NN/g, Drag-and-Drop: How to Design for Ease of Use](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/drag-drop/)
- NN/g on handles and cursors: a handle icon is a safe target that starts a drag without activating nearby controls, but has no universal convention and must not look like a kebab or hamburger menu; use the platform's standard cursors; grabbed objects need a distinct look (outline, shadow, offset, or translucent ghost). — [NN/g, Drag-and-Drop: How to Design for Ease of Use](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/drag-drop/)
- Atlassian on handles: start with the whole entity ("if an entity is draggable (eg a card), then make the whole entity draggable"), but if it contains buttons or dropdowns "just make the drag handle icon the draggable part of the entity"; making the whole entity draggable also blocks text selection in it. A handle that is the only draggable part needs a target of at least 24 px by 24 px. For secondary drags the handle may appear on hover or focus-within, at a cost in discoverability. — [Atlassian Design: design guidelines](https://atlassian.design/components/pragmatic-drag-and-drop/design-guidelines)
- Atlassian on feedback: the original stays in place at 40% opacity; the preview is not rotated and stays within 280 px by 280 px; placement is shown by a 2 px **drop indicator line**, and by a background highlight only where a drop is really possible ("We don't want to have a situation where a background color changes, but when the user drops, nothing happens."). Other items are not shifted. After the drop: optimistic update, and the moved item's background flashes for 700 ms. Menus and tooltips attached to the dragged entity close when the drag starts. — [Atlassian Design: design guidelines](https://atlassian.design/components/pragmatic-drag-and-drop/design-guidelines)
- Apple HIG: "Display a drag image as soon as people drag a selection about three points."; "It works well to create a translucent representation of the content people are dragging."; "avoid creating a distracting experience in which the drag image is constantly and radically changing."; "Show people whether a destination can accept dragged content."; "Display highlighting or other visual cues only while the content is positioned above the destination."; on failure the item can "move back from its current location to its source (if the source is still visible)"; "Scroll the contents of a destination when necessary."; "Prefer letting people undo a drag-and-drop operation."; "Offer alternative ways to accomplish drag-and-drop actions." — [Apple HIG: Drag and drop](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/drag-and-drop) (read through the page's data endpoint)
- Cloudscape on grid behaviour: equal-sized items **swap**; where a swap is not possible the dragged item **pushes** others; an optional **drop preview** "shows where the active item will land and should reflect the item's general shape and size"; an item dropped outside the layout or where there is no room returns to its original location; moving keeps an item's size, visible content and configuration. Active items get a drop shadow and an active border. — [Cloudscape: drag-and-drop pattern](https://cloudscape.design/patterns/general/drag-and-drop/), [Cloudscape: configurable dashboard](https://cloudscape.design/patterns/general/service-dashboard/configurable-dashboard/)
- Activation constraints in dnd-kit: either a `distance` in pixels the pointer must travel before a drag starts, or a `delay` in milliseconds with a `tolerance` in pixels of movement allowed during the delay (example value 5 px); the two are mutually exclusive. — [dnd-kit: pointer sensor](https://dndkit.com/api-documentation/sensors/pointer)
- Aborting: WCAG 2.5.2 expects completion on the up-event with a way to abort before completion or undo afterwards; releasing outside a drop target reverting the action is its own example. dnd-kit cancels on Escape. — [Understanding SC 2.5.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-cancellation.html), [dnd-kit accessibility guide](https://dndkit.com/guides/accessibility)
- Informal evidence on unpredictability when items move: a GNOME issue argues that a thick line is "way clearer" as a drop target, that a gap-based target makes the list jump unless transitions are animated, and that without animation users must rescan the list to understand the new order; a vendor changed from showing a drop zone for every item, which "made the screen jump around like crazy", to showing only the zone under the cursor. *(search snippets; bug reports, not studies)* — [GNOME control-center issue 655](https://gitlab.gnome.org/GNOME/gnome-control-center/-/issues/655), [Digioh help](https://help.digioh.com/?p=10675)

### Inferences
- In a grid with items of different sizes, live reflow changes the geometry under the pointer: a neighbour that moves aside may bring a different neighbour under the dragged item's centre, which triggers another reflow. NN/g's centre-crosses-edge rule reduces this in lists but does not remove it in 2D. Two stabilisers follow from the sources: compute the target from the layout as it was when the drag started (or from the last committed preview) rather than from the animating positions, and require the pointer to leave a small band before the target changes back (hysteresis). No source states this for grids; it is derived.
- The disagreement between "make room" (NN/g, Cloudscape's push and preview) and "indicator only" (Atlassian) resolves by case: where the outcome is fully described by an insertion point (a sequence with equal-sized items), a line is enough and nothing needs to move; where the outcome depends on packing (different sizes, wrapping), only a placeholder of the item's real size in its real resulting place tells the user what will happen. A placeholder that shows the final layout plus the dragged item as an overlay is what Cloudscape describes.
- Charts that are themselves dragged or zoomed, tables with text selection, and any widget with controls conflict with whole-item dragging (Atlassian's rule for interactive children; text selection blocked). On a dashboard the handle - typically the widget's header - should be the draggable part, and it is also the place for the actions menu.
- Reduced motion: reflow and snap-back animation fall under "animation from interactions" (2.3.3, AAA, listed by Vispero); honouring `prefers-reduced-motion` by cutting to the end state is the consistent reading.
- Apple's 3-point threshold, NN/g's "timing delay" for touch and dnd-kit's distance constraint all serve the same purpose: a click or tap on the handle must remain a click. This matters doubly when the handle is also a menu button (Atlassian's drag-handle menu, Cloudscape's "two pointer events within the drag handle").

### Gaps
- No controlled study comparing live reflow with a static insertion indicator was found, and no published evidence on predictability problems specific to dashboards with compaction (react-grid-layout, Gridstack); the evidence is bug reports and design opinion.
- Hysteresis, FLIP animation and auto-scroll speed near edges: no authoritative guidance found beyond Apple's one sentence on auto-scroll; Atlassian's design page covers neither auto-scroll nor cancel behaviour.
- Material Design 3 has no retrievable official guidance on reordering; only framework defaults were found (Flutter's `ReorderableListView`).
- react-beautiful-dnd's design notes on "natural movement" were not retrieved.

## 5. Touch

### Takeaway
On touch the drag competes with scrolling, and only CSS `touch-action` settles that reliably; the documented remedies are a handle that owns the gesture, a press-and-hold delay, or an explicit mode. NN/g advises using drag and drop on touch sparingly and only when testing shows users expect it and no cheaper alternative exists - which again points to the menu actions as the primary touch route, with drag as the extra.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g on touch: hover signifiers do not exist; leave at least 1 cm by 1 cm of unused space around draggable objects; fingers must not cover the feedback; distinguish taps, scrolls and grabs by a timing delay; use haptics - "one subtle bump when an object is grabbed and another when it is dragged over a drop zone"; use drag and drop on touch only when usability testing shows users expect it and no alternative with lower interaction cost (menus, cut and paste) exists; on mobile, menus can be less error-prone. — [NN/g, Drag-and-Drop: How to Design for Ease of Use](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/drag-drop/)
- dnd-kit: "Using `touch-action: none;` is the only way to reliably prevent scrolling for pointer events."; pointer-event listeners cannot block the browser's default touch behaviour; changing `touch-action` after an interaction has started has no effect on it; in scrollable lists apply `touch-action: none` only to a drag handle so the rest still scrolls; some tolerance during a press delay is advisable because touch is less precise. — [dnd-kit: pointer sensor](https://dndkit.com/api-documentation/sensors/pointer)
- WCAG 2.5.7's rationale is explicitly about touch: the requirement is separate from keyboard accessibility "because people using a touchscreen device" may be unable to drag and have no keyboard. Custom dragging that suppresses the browser's scrolling is in scope. — [Understanding SC 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)
- Target sizes: 24 by 24 CSS px minimum (WCAG 2.5.8); Atlassian's handle minimum is likewise 24 px by 24 px. — [Understanding SC 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html), [Atlassian Design: design guidelines](https://atlassian.design/components/pragmatic-drag-and-drop/design-guidelines)
- Apple HIG: "As much as possible, let people select and drag content with a single motion."; iOS and iPadOS expose drag sources and drop points to assistive technology through accessibility APIs; on touch as elsewhere, "Offer alternative ways to accomplish drag-and-drop actions." — [Apple HIG: Drag and drop](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/drag-and-drop)
- Framework defaults: Flutter's `ReorderableListView` starts a drag on long press anywhere on the item on mobile platforms and from a dedicated drag-handle icon on desktop. *(search snippet)* — [Flutter API: ReorderableListView](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/material/ReorderableListView-class.html)
- Touch screen readers: React Aria lets VoiceOver/TalkBack users swipe between valid drop targets and double-tap to drop; Vispero notes an implementation "may need to accommodate gesture navigation that's used with mobile screen readers". — [React Aria blog: drag and drop](https://react-aria.adobe.com/blog/drag-and-drop), [Vispero: The Road to Accessible Drag and Drop, Part 1](https://vispero.com/resources/the-road-to-accessible-drag-and-drop-part-1/)
- Layout on small screens in a shipping system: Cloudscape's board adapts to four breakpoints - six columns on large monitors, four on laptops, two on tablets, one on mobile. — [Cloudscape: configurable dashboard](https://cloudscape.design/patterns/general/service-dashboard/configurable-dashboard/)

### Inferences
- An explicit edit mode resolves the scroll/drag conflict on tablets better than long-press alone: outside the mode nothing is draggable and the page scrolls everywhere; inside it, the handle (with `touch-action: none`) drags at once and the rest of the widget still scrolls. Long-press on a widget's body collides with text selection, context menus and the charts' own gestures.
- Because `touch-action` cannot be changed once a touch has begun, the handle must carry `touch-action: none` before the gesture starts; a design in which the whole widget becomes draggable only after a long press needs touch events with `preventDefault` in `touchmove` instead (the alternative dnd-kit points to) or accepts that the first movement scrolls.
- With one or two columns (phones, small tablets) a 2D arrangement degenerates to a list; "move earlier/later" remains meaningful there while "move left/right" does not - another argument for sequence-based commands.
- 24 px is the conformance floor, not a touch recommendation; NN/g's 1 cm spacing figure and the platforms' larger touch targets argue for handles and menu buttons well above it on touch.

### Gaps
- The iOS/iPadOS home screen's edit mode ("jiggle mode": touch and hold, drag, resize handle, remove, Done) is the best-known precedent for an explicit mode on touch, but Apple's page could not be read in this run, and Apple's HIG page as fetched gives no explicit touch-and-hold guidance.
- Platform touch-target figures (Apple 44 pt, Material 48 dp, WCAG 2.5.5's 44 px) were not fetched.
- Haptics on the web: no source checked for the Vibration API's availability (notably on iOS Safari) in 2026.
- No study of dashboard arrangement on tablets was found.

## 6. Edit mode and error recovery

### Takeaway
The guidance converges on reversibility over prevention: provide undo "to great lengths", keep confirmation for what cannot be undone, and make any mode unmistakable. Whether arranging should need an explicit edit mode is not settled by evidence; shipping dashboard tools mostly use one, and NN/g's account of modes gives the trade: fewer accidental changes against lower discoverability and mode slips. An "Undo" that lives only in a self-dismissing toast is contested under WCAG 2.2.1.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g on modes (Page Laubheimer, 14 April 2019): modes mean "Same input, different results."; "Mode slips happen because the system doesn't clearly indicate its status to the user."; features available only in one mode are harder to discover; "we strongly recommend at least two visual indicators" of the active mode; avoid modes where a slip is unsafe; spring-loaded modes prevent slips but are less discoverable and less efficient for long sequences. Modes are useful where there are more functions than input types - its example is one touchscreen drag meaning both scrolling and drawing. — [NN/g, Modes in User Interfaces](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modes/)
- NN/g on confirmation versus undo (Jakob Nielsen, 18 February 2018, reviewed 7 August 2026): confirmations belong before serious or irreversible actions; used routinely they train users to click "Yes" without reading (the boy who cried wolf); designers should "do go to great lengths to provide undo"; a dialog must restate the action and its consequence and label buttons with outcomes ("Delete file" / "Keep file"). — [NN/g, Confirmation Dialogs Can Prevent User Errors - If Not Overused](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/)
- Apple HIG: "Prefer letting people undo a drag-and-drop operation."; ask for confirmation before an operation that cannot be undone; where undo is impossible offer another way to reverse the result. — [Apple HIG: Drag and drop](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/drag-and-drop)
- WCAG 2.5.2: a pointer action completes on the up-event and can be aborted before completion or undone after it. — [Understanding SC 2.5.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-cancellation.html)
- Cloudscape on removing a widget: "If a user removed an item they configured, follow the delete with simple confirmation pattern. Otherwise, allow removal without confirmation."; removed items return to the palette so they can be added back; do not show an alert in the layout as feedback for a removal; the remove action sits in the item's dropdown menu; "Preserve users' configurations for future visits." — [Cloudscape: configurable dashboard](https://cloudscape.design/patterns/general/service-dashboard/configurable-dashboard/)
- Products with an explicit editing mode: Power BI's move and resize shortcuts carry the footnote "Must be in editing mode"; Grafana documents `Esc` to "Exit full-screen panel or edit mode" and `Ctrl+S` to save the dashboard, that is, a draft that is saved explicitly. — [Microsoft Learn: Keyboard shortcuts in Power BI Desktop](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-bi/create-reports/desktop-accessibility-keyboard-shortcuts), [Grafana docs: use dashboards](https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/latest/dashboards/use-dashboards/)
- Atlassian: update the interface optimistically on drop and persist asynchronously. — [Atlassian Design: design guidelines](https://atlassian.design/components/pragmatic-drag-and-drop/design-guidelines)
- Undo in a toast: GitLab's design team holds that an "Undo" action inside an auto-dismissing toast can fail 2.2.1 because keyboard and screen-reader users may not reach it in time, and wants critical actions available on the page without a time limit; Scott O'Hara notes screen-reader users must find the action before the message disappears; on the WAI interest-group list (2025) one contributor says toasts "almost always fall afoul of WCAG 2.2.1" while another holds that notification-only toasts do not; WCAG issue #1814 on the scope of timed content is unresolved. *(search snippets)* — [GitLab design.gitlab.com issue 2771](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-org/gitlab-services/design.gitlab.com/-/issues/2771), [Scott O'Hara, A toast to a11y toasts](https://scottohara.me/blog/2019/07/08/a-toast-to-a11y-toasts.html), [w3c-wai-ig, 2025](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/w3c-wai-ig/2025AprJun/0083.html), [w3c/wcag #1814](https://github.com/w3c/wcag/issues/1814)
- Defaults matter more than customisation: in Jared Spool's analysis of several hundred users' Microsoft Word settings files, fewer than 5% had changed any of 150+ settings (14 September 2011). — [UIE: Do users change their settings?](https://archive.uie.com/brainsparks/2011/09/14/do-users-change-their-settings/)

### Inferences
- The two axes are independent and are often conflated: (1) is arranging always available or only in a mode; (2) are changes applied at once with undo, or held in a draft until Save. Mode + draft (Grafana, Power BI) gives Cancel for the whole session and protects against accidental drags; always-editable + autosave + undo gives the least friction but needs a persistent undo and makes accidental drags on touch likely.
- For end users arranging their own dashboard, the sources support: an explicit, clearly indicated arrange mode (two indicators, per NN/g) as the default on touch and wherever widget content is interactive; inside it, every action applied immediately and reversible; leaving the mode saves. A Cancel that discards the whole session is cheap if the library keeps the arrangement as one value: it is restoring the previous value. The menu actions (move, size, remove) can remain available outside the mode, since they are deliberate single activations and not gestures that happen by accident.
- Removing a widget: no confirmation where the widget carries no configuration of its own (Cloudscape, NN/g); instead undo, plus the widget's reappearance in the catalogue. Where the widget carries options the user set, removing must not lose them silently - either keep the options with the undo, or confirm with the consequence named.
- Undo must not live only in a toast that times out. Conforming options: an Undo control that stays on the page (for instance in the arrange-mode toolbar) for as long as the mode lasts; or Ctrl/Cmd+Z plus a toast that does not dismiss while it has focus or hover; and the removal announced through `role=status`.
- Reset to the default arrangement is itself a destructive action on the user's own work and is the case where a specific confirmation ("Reset the arrangement? Your 9 widgets return to the default layout.") or an undo is warranted.
- Since most users never customise (Spool), the default arrangement is the product for the majority; edit affordances must not tax those who never use them, which favours a mode entered on request over permanently visible handles.

### Gaps
- No study was found that compares an explicit edit mode with always-editable dashboards, or draft-with-Save against autosave-with-undo, for arrangement tasks. NN/g's modes article does not compare them directly.
- NN/g guidance specific to "unsaved changes" patterns was not retrieved.
- How Datadog, Azure Portal, Salesforce and Home Assistant handle accidental widget removal was not verified.
- Spool's figure is from 2011 and concerns Word settings, not dashboards; no adoption figure for dashboard customisation was found.

## 7. Empty states, first run, loading and failing widgets

### Takeaway
An empty dashboard must say that it is empty by design, teach what a widget is, and offer the action that fills it; a widget that is loading, empty or failed must say which of the three it is. NN/g recommends a spinner for a single dashboard card and reserves skeletons for full-page loads; nothing needs an indicator under one second.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g, "Designing Empty States in Complex Applications: 3 Guidelines" (Kate Kaplan, 19 September 2021): (1) communicate system status - a totally empty area leaves users unsure whether the system is working, loading or has failed; never show "no records" while content is still loading; (2) provide learning cues in context instead of forced tutorials (Datadog's empty favourites area tells users to star items); (3) provide direct pathways for key tasks - a "Create" button and a "Learn more" link, or a second path such as exploring with demo data (Loggly). Dashboards and workspaces are named as common sources of empty states. — [NN/g, Designing Empty States in Complex Applications](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/)
- NN/g, "Skeleton Screens 101" (Samhita Tankala, 4 June 2023, reviewed 2 September 2026): under 1 second no indicator is needed and one can make a quick load feel jarring; 2-10 seconds a spinner or skeleton; over 10 seconds a progress bar with an explicit estimate of duration. Skeleton screens are for full-page loads; spinners suit a single module, "such as a video or a dashboard card". Animated skeletons (shimmer, pulse) can distract and cause accessibility problems; a skeleton that shows only the frame can make users think the page is broken. — [NN/g, Skeleton Screens 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/)
- Cloudscape Board: an `empty` slot is rendered when there are no items and may also carry a loading indicator; the component does not manage loading, error and loaded states, the client does, including the ARIA-live announcements for them. — [cloudscape-design/board-components, interfaces.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cloudscape-design/board-components/main/src/board/interfaces.ts)
- Cloudscape's add flow: an **Add item** button shows or hides a palette in a split panel; each palette entry has a title and a description; users add by dragging onto the board; the palette can be filtered, and an empty filter result shows a message and a clear-filter action; removed items return to the palette. — [Cloudscape: configurable dashboard](https://cloudscape.design/patterns/general/service-dashboard/configurable-dashboard/)
- A newly inserted item takes a declared default size (`defaultRowSpan`/`defaultColumnSpan`), not smaller than its minimum. — [cloudscape-design/board-components, interfaces.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cloudscape-design/board-components/main/src/board/interfaces.ts)
- Announcing results: adding and removing are results of an action in the sense of 4.1.3 and belong in `role=status`. — [Understanding SC 4.1.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html)

### Inferences
- Three empty states must be told apart: the dashboard has no widgets (teach and offer "Add widget"), a widget has no data (say so, in the widget), and a widget has not loaded or has failed (status or error with retry, in the widget). A failed widget should still be movable, resizable and removable; the frame with its header and menu must render independently of the content.
- Cloudscape's palette is drag-only as described on the pattern page; under 2.5.7 and 2.1.1 the catalogue needs an activation route as well: choosing an entry adds the widget at the end of the sequence (or at a declared default place), moves focus or scrolls to it, and announces "Revenue added at position 9 of 9".
- While widgets load, the placeholder should have the widget's final size so that the grid does not reflow when content arrives; per NN/g a spinner or a calm placeholder inside each widget frame is preferable to a shimmer across the board.
- A first run should not be empty where a sensible default arrangement exists (Spool's defaults finding); the empty state is then the state after the user removed everything, and it should offer both "Add widget" and "Restore default".

### Gaps
- No authoritative guidance specific to a failed widget (error message, retry, partial failure of a dashboard) was retrieved; NN/g's empty-state article gives no error-state guidance. Carbon, PatternFly and Polaris were not consulted.
- Cloudscape's own empty-state and dashboard-item pages were not read.
- No evidence on onboarding for customisable dashboards (coach marks versus contextual cues) beyond NN/g's general preference for contextual cues.

## 8. Published research on end-user dashboard customisation interactions

### Takeaway
Direct research is scarce: no study was found that measures dragging to arrange tiles, grid snapping or resizing by end users on dashboards. What exists is adjacent - customisation of dashboard content can help but only when deliberate, dragging is intrinsically slower and more error-prone than pointing, few people change defaults, and GitHub's testing with disabled users favours an explicit move dialog.

### Cited Findings
- Alsayahani, Alhamadi, Harper, Vigo, "The Effects of Customisation on the Usability of Visual Analytics Dashboards: the Good, the Bad, and the Ugly", ACM IUI 2025, pp. 1426-1439, DOI 10.1145/3708359.3712120: studies global filters, hiding of visualisations and switching of graph types; "time constraints" and lack of awareness of customisation functions are barriers; short-term surface customisations made users faster and more accurate with lower reported cognitive load, but only when engagement was meaningful (lasting more than 5 seconds); some customisations, not carefully implemented, were detrimental to cognitive load and completion time. — [University of Manchester Research Explorer](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effects-of-customisation-on-the-usability-of-visual-analytics/)
- "Behavioural Indicators of Usability in Visual Analytics Dashboards": extensive exploration following customisation was associated with higher perceived performance; its literature review notes customisation's complexity can be daunting for non-experts and lead to cluttered designs. *(search snippet)* — [ACM DOI 10.1145/3715710](https://doi.org/10.1145/3715710)
- Bach et al., "Dashboard Design Patterns" (IEEE VIS 2022): personalisation by adding, moving or resizing widgets is described as a common approach. *(search snippet)* — [ResearchGate: Dashboard Design Patterns](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/363869631_Dashboard_Design_Patterns)
- MacKenzie, Sellen, Buxton (CHI '91): dragging versus pointing with a mouse - 916 ms versus 674 ms, 10.8% versus 3.5% errors; worse with a trackball (17.3% errors). — [MacKenzie, Sellen, Buxton 1991](https://www.yorku.ca/mack/CHI91.html)
- Spool (2011): fewer than 5% of several hundred Word users had changed any setting; programmers and designers were the exception, often changing over 40% of options. — [UIE: Do users change their settings?](https://archive.uie.com/brainsparks/2011/09/14/do-users-change-their-settings/)
- GitHub (2024), user testing with people who use assistive technology: rapid keyboard moves produced stale announcements that in-house testing had missed; first-time users needed instructions; many users preferred the move dialog over drag emulation "reporting greater confidence". — [GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/engineering/user-experience/exploring-the-challenges-in-creating-an-accessible-sortable-list-drag-and-drop/)
- NN/g (2020) recommends observing in usability tests whether users attempt to drag, without asking them, before adopting drag and drop. — [NN/g, Drag-and-Drop: How to Design for Ease of Use](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/drag-drop/)

### Inferences
- The Manchester result concerns what a dashboard shows, not where its widgets sit, but its two conditions transfer: customisation pays when the user is aware of it and engages deliberately, and a careless implementation costs time and attention. That supports a visible entry point to arranging and a low-effort interaction once inside.
- MacKenzie's error rates (roughly one drag in ten missing its target with a mouse, more with other devices) are the quantitative basis for making every drop reversible and for offering non-drag routes to everyone, not only to users of assistive technology.
- Spool's exception (technical users change a lot) suggests customisation use will be bimodal; the design has to serve the majority that never arranges and the minority that arranges heavily.

### Gaps
- No published usability study or paper was found that measures end users arranging tiles by drag, grid snapping, compaction behaviour or resizing on dashboards, in 2026 or earlier. The search was one extended query and the related results it surfaced; ACM DL and IEEE Xplore were not searched directly.
- The Manchester paper's participant count and its breakdown of which customisations were "bad" and "ugly" are not in the abstract; the full text was not read.
- The two search-snippet papers (DOI 10.1145/3715710; Bach et al.) were not opened.
